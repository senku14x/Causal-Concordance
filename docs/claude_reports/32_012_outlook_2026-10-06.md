## 0. Bottom line

1. **Use the ST multihop bank.** All 96 items are WSB multihop items with identical prompts and bridges. 49 of them are in the test half that 007 read. phrase-lens adds a ready-made swap target (`tgt`) and its answer (`y_b`).
2. **Use the MT bank only after fixing it.**
   - It is phrase-lens's own, Claude-drafted, and none of its 67 items are WSB items.
   - 16 items carry flags for real relation errors.
   - It is still more usable than WSB's `multihop_mt`, whose bridges are obscure 3–5-token entities ("Simula 67"). Building counterfactual targets with known answers there would be hard.
3. **Multi-token swaps are not easier; they are harder.**
   - There are no J rows.
   - Phrase-Jacobian (PJ) directions are finite-sample estimates that nobody has hosted or validated.
   - Answers run to 2–5 tokens.
   - The repo ships no results showing that any of its swaps work.
4. **PJ directions are not needed for most of this.**
   - Model-side labels and the paper's privilege test work for multi-token concepts through class-mean ("probe") directions, which need no lens.
   - PJ is only needed to give multi-token concepts a lens row of their own.
5. **First step:** a cheap lever check, then stop and report. Run J-row swaps at L24–47 at every position on ST, with an answer-swap control and phrase-lens's src-ablation control. If there is no lever on Qwen multihop at the mid band, most of 012 is moot.
6. **Three problems in the draft would invalidate results:**
   - every reader read sits inside the edited band;
   - the mid band cannot run under SEC's float32 replay as written;
   - the PJ gate would probably fail by construction, and it is a hard stop.

## 1. What phrase-lens contains (checked)

**Included:**
- 14 frozen banks:
  - 10 readout banks (byte-for-byte WSB copies);
  - causal multihop ST (96) and MT (67);
  - broadcast ST and MT (192 each).
- The template vocabulary (13,389 entries) and the causal vocabulary (283 cased entries).
- The passage prompt and an artifact manifest.
- Method code with CPU tests.

**Not included:**
- Any fitted tensors: template (8.2 GiB), PJ and anchored banks (about 16.3 GiB each), and the causal PJ bank.
- The fitting corpus.
- **Any results.** Nothing in the repo shows that its swaps flip Qwen's answers. The writeup would; that is the main reason to read it.

**J-lens:** the same Neuronpedia file and revision as our J_NP (`0731326e`, `…_n1000.pt`).

**Its swap:** the same primitive as our 008 clamp.
- V = [v_src, v_tgt], unit rows, raw coordinates.
- c_clean = V⁺ h_clean, then h ← h + α V (swap(c_clean) − V⁺ h).
- Applied at every band layer (default L24–47) and every prompt position, on the prefill pass only, α = 1, no whitening.
- A pair whose src and tgt directions have cosine > 0.999 is a recorded no-op and counts as a failure.

**Its random control:** keeps the true src direction and replaces the target with a random direction. That removes src and injects random content. It is a sharper control than isotropic random pairs; we should run both.

**Its gate:** a chat-templated question about the *counterfactual* fact (greedy strict match, and ≥ 8/10 generous matches at T 0.7). The intervention prompt itself was never gated.

**The ST bank in numbers:**
- 36 relation keys, uneven (capital 16, language 9, continent 6, …).
- 30 of 96 prompts end in a space.
- 14 of 96 have a multi-token src in the leading-space form. Numerals split into digits (" 6" is 2 tokens, but the bare "6" is one); San Francisco, New Zealand, polar bear, Sherlock Holmes, Los Angeles, New York and octagon have no single-token form.

**The MT bank in numbers:**
- 6 of 67 items have a single-token src.
- There is no family field, so clusters must be defined.
- 16 items are flagged for scrutiny, including:
  - "Caspian" given as an ocean;
  - krill given as marine mammals;
  - "…is the" followed by "Spain";
  - a bare "plymouth".

**Read position:**
- WSB multihop reads the *last word token*: offset −2 when the prompt ends in a space, −1 otherwise. 007 Stage 3 used this.
- phrase-lens reads the *final rendered token*, which is a bare space on those 30 prompts.
- **Use WSB's position.** It keeps us comparable with 007's reads and avoids reading a space token.

**Collisions:** no src/tgt pair in either bank shares a first token.

## 2. Do we need phrase-Jacobian (PJ) directions?

**What a PJ direction is.** It is the multi-token analogue of a J-lens row: the gradient of the summed raw logits of the teacher-forced phrase, averaged over generic contexts. phrase-lens uses 128 WikiText contexts per phrase. It gives "San Francisco" a direction of its own, where the J-lens only has rows for " San" and " Francisco". The repo describes the method but doesn't ship the directions, so we would compute them ourselves.

**When we need them:** only to swap or read multi-token concepts *through a lens row*, i.e. the multi-token version of "J rows as a handle".

**When we don't:**
- **Model-side labels and the privilege test.** The paper's §3.3 test (Fig. 16) builds each intermediate's direction as a class mean: the average activation over prompts that imply the concept through different cues, minus the mean over other concepts. That works for any concept, however many tokens it has. Its J part comes from sparse pursuit over ordinary single-token J rows, so multi-token concepts decompose into whatever atoms fit, e.g. san, francisco, california, bay.
- **Readers.** Readers don't use J rows at all.

**Cost if we do want them:** fitting is the most expensive item in the draft. About 150 MT concepts × 128 contexts of backward passes through a 27B model comes to roughly 3–10 GPU-h; this is a rough guess, to be measured on 5 phrases first.

**One known problem:** a PJ for a single token is not the same quantity as the published J row.
- PJ differentiates the raw logit (so it includes the final norm) from one end position.
- The J-lens averages over all (t, t′ ≥ t) pairs.

So the draft's gate, cos(PJ, J row) ≥ 0.9 at every band layer, may fail by construction. Calibrate it against PJ's own split-half cosine instead.

**Recommendation:** treat PJ as optional, after the lever check. Use class-mean directions for multi-token concepts first.

## 4. Fixing the prompt set

1. **Gate the actual intervention prompt:** greedy y_a correct, ≥ 9/10 at T 0.8, and the bare prompt does not already give y_b. phrase-lens only gated the counterfactual fact, through chat.
2. **Add a counterfactual prompt per item** that implies tgt and gives y_b, token-aligned with the original where possible (atomic number 26 → 29; May → August). Gate it. It makes three things possible:
   - patch labels;
   - a "real tgt state" reference for the readers (a reader naming tgt on the real tgt prompt is the positive control for the swapped reads);
   - a check that swapped states look like real tgt states.
3. **Fix or drop the 20 flagged items** (4 ST, 16 MT).
   - Replace tgt with a concept of the right type, or reword the relation: an ocean-adjacent city instead of the Caspian; prey that are marine mammals instead of krill; a country that fits "is the"; an unambiguous Plymouth.
   - Re-gate every fixed item and keep a record of each change. Unfixable items go to a separate table.
4. **The 30 trailing-space ST prompts:** keep WSB's render, so 007's reads stay comparable. Read at `last_word_token` and score bare answer spellings. A stripped-prompt run is optional, as a sensitivity check.
5. **Spellings:**
   - Record the exact token ids of every accepted spelling.
   - Check the alternatives lists; MT answers run to 5 tokens ("J. K. Rowling").
   - Score m by log-sum-exp over all spellings and every answer token, as in our margin code.
6. **Mark the 14 ST items with a multi-token src.**
   - For numerals, the J row is the bare single-token fallback ("6"), a different surface form from the prompt's; report those items separately.
   - The others ("San Francisco", …) need a class-mean direction, or PJ.
7. **Clusters:**
   - ST: the 36 relation keys; report per-cluster results (capital is 16 of 96).
   - MT: assign a family by relation, by hand, and record it.
8. **New material for the Fig. 16 replication:** about 10–16 cue-varied prompts per concept, each gated to show the model resolves the concept.

## 5. Other problems in the 012 draft

1. **Every reader read is inside the edited band.** The Oracle reads L40/L44 and the NLA L42, all inside L24–47, so a reader naming tgt may simply be reading the coordinates the clamp wrote. This repeats 005 v1's "near-direct read". **Fix:** read downstream of the edit: either edit L24–47 and read the Oracle at L52/L56, or edit L24–35 and read at L40/L44. The NLA reads only at L42, so it needs the second option.
2. **SEC and the mid band.** SEC's float32 replay starts at block 40. Moving it down to block 24 probably doesn't fit in 96 GB; check before assuming. Otherwise run the mid band under the RHUT float32-residual regime and measure the noise floor (bf16 floor up to about 0.2 nats). The 1-nat threshold should still clear it. Say this in the spec.
3. **The PJ gate** (§2 above): calibrate it, and don't make it a hard stop for the whole experiment.
4. **R2 (NLA-reconstructor vectors): drop it, or run it only after a lever is shown.**
   - The NLA reconstructor maps full NLA explanations to an L42 state, so "The text is about {c}" is far off its input distribution.
   - It edits a single layer.
   - A null from it is uninformative.
   - Its gate cites reconstruction metrics from 001/002 that may not exist; check.
5. **R1's G-AR needs definitions.**
   - Fix the retrieval pool size; the config's `micro_batch` is 24. Our loader got 0.86 within batches of 24 and 0.38 over 768.
   - The output-norm gate assumes an MSE-trained head, but the AR's loss is unknown (`loss_mix_lambda`).
6. **Route B (training our own reconstructor) is a bad idea; strike it.** It isn't the Oracle's ruler, it adds a new unvalidated instrument, and WSB trained theirs on 277M tokens across 8 GPUs.
7. **Two decision-table rows don't follow.**
   - **"Readers name src after a working swap → re-solving from the cue."** The swap edits a 2-dim span and leaves most of the src content in the orthogonal complement (the paper puts the J part at 6–16% of variance). A faithful reader of the whole vector should still see src. 007 gives a sharp prediction instead: **the Oracle, which depends on J, should follow swaps more than the NLA, which does not.** Write this down before the run; it is the most informative output of the reader stage.
   - **"Same J pattern on used and unused items → J dependence is a reader property."** "Used" is defined by J-row swaps working, and swap success tracks how strongly the bridge loads on its J row (the paper's own result). So items will differ in reader J-pattern by construction. Condition on loading, or define "used" by a non-J lever (class mean, whole-cell patch).
8. **Process:**
   - "No compute limits / never cut a condition" works against information gain. PJ fits alone exceed the 2 GPU-h check-in line.
   - §9 chains smoke → ST → MT, against the stop-after-smoke rule.
   - "NLA at L44" is an off-contract read.
   - "Stage 6 (broadcast)" is referenced but never defined.
   - Hand-reading 40 readouts per reader per condition comes to thousands; limit it to the key conditions.

**Sound and worth keeping:**
- gating the actual prompt;
- the output-facing covariate as a measurement, not a gate;
- treating lens rows as covectors and reconstructor outputs as displacements in the whitened swap;
- nothing goes under Camila's name before G-AR passes;
- the "Not claimed" section.
