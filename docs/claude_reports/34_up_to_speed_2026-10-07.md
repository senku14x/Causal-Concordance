## (a) The project and 012, in my own words

**The project.** Causal-Concordance asks whether activation readers agree on, and track, what Qwen3.6-27B demonstrably uses.
- **Readers:**
  - token lenses: J_NP, J_CB, logit;
  - the Oracle Lens: WorkspaceBench's reproduction of the paper's A.9.2 oracle lens, read at L40/L44 on contract;
  - the NLA: nla-rl `av_base` plus `iter_000400`, read at L42.
- **Labels of "used"** come from interventions on the model alone. Readers never enter the labels.

**The paper claims at stake.**
- J-space components carry verbal report and multihop intermediates (Fig. 8: 59% vs 5%; Fig. 16: 61% vs 28%, then 6% under the clamp).
- J-lens vectors are a privileged format that downstream circuits read from.

**001–011 in brief.**
- Model-side J privilege has not reproduced on Qwen:
  - 005: planted concepts give lexical priming, not report;
  - 008: on brew, the J part moves the answer by S 0.02–0.03, against 0.21 for the non-J part.
- Reader-side J dependence is reader-specific (007): the Oracle's z tracking at brew `:` L40 is carried by J; the NLA's is not.
- Readers' tracking is repeatedly matched by the model's own output (006b, 009).

**What 012 did.** It moved to a family where the paper's lever actually works: Jan Chen's ST multihop bank (96 WSB items plus swap targets; 82 clean-correct items analysed).
- **The lever.**
  - Every-position J-row coordinate swaps change the answer on 48/82 items at L24–47 and 38/82 at L24–35.
  - The random-target controls flip 5/82 and 1/82; logit-row swaps flip 0/82.
  - The swap is a reflection of the src−tgt contrast, so its direction is set by the clean state. Edits that move toward src almost never work (2/18 at L24–35; 0/14 at L24–47).
- **Readers at the answer token.** Every reader's src→tgt shift tracks which swaps worked (AUC 0.88–0.97). But the model's own next-token output at that token tracks them just as well (0.97 bridge, 0.96 answer, at L24–35). The readers share one signal, and there is no detectable Oracle–NLA difference.
- **Where the effect is built.** Editing every position except the read token flips 35/82; editing only the read token flips 0/82.
- **Earlier positions.** At the written early positions, readers detect the write whether or not the model uses it.
- **The delayed-question variant (I/J) gave the cleanest model-side result.**
  - Swapping only the clue tokens changes the later answer, whichever of three questions follows: 36/60 trials, against 0/60 for random.
  - The hold states are bitwise identical across the questions.
  - At the carrier end, a token that holds the country but is neither written nor output-facing, no reader sees anything (AUC about 0.47).
  - At the clue end, readers reach 0.75–0.81, no better than the logit lens, and the model's own next-token output there already gives 0.69, above the pre-set 0.65 bar.

**Net (exploratory; Qwen3.6-27B, these banks, read cells and bands).**
- Readers do track J-derived interventions that the model acts on.
- In every setting tested, that tracking is either shared with output-facing content or is detection of the written edit.
- No paired Oracle-vs-NLA difference has been detected anywhere in 012.

**Two limits on how far this goes.**
1. **The labels are J-derived** (audit issue 4). They show "an answer effect under this J intervention". They are not an independent standard for ranking J-based readers, and they are not natural mediation.
2. **"Readers see nothing at the hold token" is not yet evidence of absence.**
   - The model's own next-token output at H2 predicts hold success (I: 0.82; J: 0.70), while every reader at L40–44 is at chance (0.46–0.49 in J).
   - So something about the swapped country is present at H2 by the final layer, but not readable at L40–44. It may arrive late, by attention from the clue tokens.
   - Single-cell mid-layer reads may simply be blind to content stored in context. I have not seen this layer gap stated in the docs; it is a cheap thing to check (see (e), optional).

2. **The specificity rule changed.**

Before pre-registering the main test I'd raise two design points:
- **"Claims the prompt doesn't support" is not the same as "hallucinated".**
  - An activation can carry true inferences that the prompt does not state; that is exactly workspace content.
  - On 012 multihop the bridge is prompt-inferable, so the conflation is small there. It would matter on the hidden-attribute family.
  - The judge's rewrite rule should separate "contradicted by prompt or response" from "unsupported".
- **Any deletion lowers FVE.** The cleaned-vs-original contrast needs a length-matched random-claim deletion control on the same explanations; otherwise an FVE drop means only "less text".
- Cost of the main run: about 520 readouts × 2 versions through the AR, around 0.1–0.2 GPU-h, plus the judge (cents to a few dollars on Gemini Flash).

**2. Systematic readout content labelling (§3.2). 0 GPU; judge spend small.**
- Fixed categories; a seeded sample per method × condition; the judge validated by hand on about 30.
- It can run on CPU while the GPU is busy. It turns the Q/D hand-reads ("the Oracle names the answer; the NLA is boilerplate") into counts with denominators.

**3. Hidden-user-attribute prompt family: spec only, no runs (§3.4).**
I'd write two lessons from 012 into the spec from the start:
- a **lever that does not come from J** (class-mean or natural state transfer), so the labels can rank J-based readers (audit issue 4);
- an **output-facing check at every read cell, including late layers**, with "beyond output" judged by a paired partial against the model's own output, not by requiring the output check to fail. Any nameable intermediate leaks into the output tail (009, I, J), so the "AUC < 0.65" gate may be unreachable by construction.
- If it passes vetting, the de-risk (gate plus lever) would be about 0.5–1 GPU-h.
