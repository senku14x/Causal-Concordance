Independent Codex audit, 2026-10-07. Repository and canonical outputs were read-only. No GPU use, project workflows, dependency changes or experiment reruns. ## Verdict

**Stage F1 remains informative, but the headline Stage F2 NLA finding does not survive applying the prespecified position mask consistently.** The model-output comparator and the readers were averaged over different positions. The omitted model position contains substantial outcome-predictive signal. This accounts for the apparent NLA association beyond the model-output baseline without a new experiment.

The highest-information next action is for Claude to repair the metadata join in canonical CPU analysis and update the interpretation, not to launch a fresh-data replication of the current 0.72/0.54 headline. The raw captures and likelihood rows already contain what is needed.

## What is wrong, and confidence in the diagnosis

Spec §13 excludes the token immediately before the read token when the prompt ends in ` is`. `stageF_analysis.py:82` implements this through `is_read_minus_1`. The saved `f2_model.jsonl` rows contain that flag and `n_prompt`; the saved reader rows in both `f2_ll_nla.jsonl` and `f2_ll_oracle_lenses.jsonl` contain neither.

The same `keep()` function is applied to the two schemas. It excludes the predecessor for the model but silently retains it for readers because the absent flag defaults to a non-true value. Consequently, the reader statistic and the output covariates do not describe the same set of cells. The ledger identifies a cosmetic plotting problem from absent `n_prompt`, but the absent exclusion flag causes an additional substantive error.

This affects 52 of 82 clean-correct items, including 40 of the 64 toward-target items used for F2. The latter cohort has 36 successful swaps and 28 unsuccessful swaps across 27 relation clusters. The partial correlation uses 63 items because one lacks the answer-token contrast.

Confidence is high: the diagnosis follows directly from the row schemas and the selection code. An independent implementation reproduced the published point estimates exactly, then joined `(item, pos)` to the saved model metadata and applied one common mask.

## Stage F1: a result that survives this issue

Independent recomputation from `f1_items.jsonl` gives:

| J intervention, L24–35 | Successful flips | Median margin change |
|---|---:|---:|
| All positions | 38/82 | 5.0454477351 nats |
| All but the read position | 35/82 | 4.4172887504 nats |
| Read position only | 0/82 | 0.1390272975 nats |

All 35 all-but-read successes are also all-position successes; the latter adds three. This supports the claim that direct editing of the answer-producing cell is unnecessary for most observed flips under this intervention, and insufficient on its own at this dose/band. It does not show the read cell itself is unnecessary: it remains on the downstream computational path. Nor does the split identify which earlier cells mediate bridge reasoning.

Readers' shifts at the unwritten cell are responses to states produced after upstream edits, not merely the exact coordinates written directly into that cell. This is a meaningful improvement over directly reading an imposed coordinate. It remains compatible with propagation of a written signal and with answer-facing information. F1 is unaffected by the F2 metadata problem.

The observation that read-only edits shift readers without changing greedy answers is also useful, but 'detection of the write itself' should remain a scoped description. Lack of a flip is not proof of no influence; the median margin change is small and nonzero.

## What the early-position model-output baseline can and cannot establish

At an early prompt position, the final-layer distribution predicts the next *prompt token*, not the eventual answer. Later prompt tokens are supplied rather than generated. A bridge/answer-tail contrast at that position is a diagnostic of local output accessibility, not a direct measure of the later answer computation or a causal mediator of it.

Therefore an early-output AUC near chance would only show that this particular averaged local output statistic does not predict eventual swap success. It would not establish that these cells are free of output-facing information, that no richer output summary could predict success, or that reader associations are bridge-specific. The present mismatch is even more concrete: the output comparator did predict success when evaluated at the same positions as the reader.

All these early cells were directly written by the intervention. Their state changes can expose source/target identities supplied by the experimenter before the prompt prefix itself specifies a bridge. Reader sensitivity there is not automatically evidence for model-computed intermediate information. Also, F2 controls edit size measured at the read position, whereas it analyzes earlier positions; it does not directly control the realized local dose at each early cell.

## Is Stage R justified?

Not as the next test of the headline claim. First use the existing data to repair Stage F's common-mask analysis. No model or reader rerun is needed for that correction.

Stage R's broadcast prompts could still support a separate exploratory generalization study, but they are not a direct replication: the source concept is explicit, the family changes, and the broadcast bank's model-side intervention behavior was already examined in Stage A. New reader measurements on that bank should not be called untouched-data confirmation. Its AUC threshold is also lowered from F2's 0.75 positive criterion to 0.65.

The proposed 'NLA-specific if others fail the test' rule is statistically invalid as a reader-specificity criterion. A future such claim needs a paired NLA-minus-Oracle contrast on the same items and a fixed endpoint, alongside each reader's positive control. A failure on the changed family could not settle the multihop question anyway, as the spec recognizes.
