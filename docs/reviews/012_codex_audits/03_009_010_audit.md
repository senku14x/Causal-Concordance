# Independent audit: experiments 009 and 010

Date: 2026-10-06. Role: independent scientific review, repository read-only. No experiment was launched, dependency changed, canonical artifact written, or held-out item opened. All recomputations used saved development outputs. This report is the only durable artifact created by this reviewer.

## Assessment

009 decisively falsifies the proposed operational shortcut: changing the correct response from a colour to its arbitrary code does not eliminate colour identity from Qwen3.6-27B's answer-position distribution. The failed reader go/no-go should remain a failed gate. It does not show that an output-independent benchmark is impossible, that the model takes a shortcut around the intermediate, or that readers are actually using output information.

010 supports a more specific observational claim: on these brew development pairs, Oracle colour-discrimination strengths covary substantially with J-lens discrimination, more than with the subject's scalar colour-discrimination statistic. NLA agreement is weaker and strongly prefix-dependent. The analysis does not establish causal use, a shared mechanism, or independence from output-facing information. Linear partial correlation against one scalar D is not a general output-information control.

The strongest existing-data next step is to evaluate whether the reader adds predictive information about a reader-free causal outcome beyond simple saved baselines, with table-held-out validation and explicit limits of the causal outcome. Merely extending agreement analysis or fitting an in-sample output covariate will not settle the North Star.

These numerically support the stopping decision by a wide margin. The five listed criteria include duplicate z/f tests because z=f at depth 1; they are not five independent failures. The conclusion is nevertheless secure because rank and counterfactual D independently establish the operational failure.

D cancels a common multiplicative factor applied to all candidate colour probabilities within each render. Therefore tiny total colour mass does not refute colour discrimination. This is the precise useful meaning of “scale-free”; D is not invariant to temperature or arbitrary changes in the colour score geometry.

The continuation result is conditional on teacher-forcing the answer and suppressing EOS for 24 tokens. It is evidence about that forced continuation, not the natural rate of spontaneous colour explanation. The answer-slot D/rank failure does not depend on interpreting forced explanations as natural behavior.

### What the benchmark has and has not established

The final-code task mathematically depends on the colour reached after a stir; the start-code task does not. That is a difference in the task's dependency graph. It does not demonstrate that the model implements a distinct intermediate variable or that a particular cell naturally uses it.

At depth 1, z is also f. This makes 009 less rich than the intended two-stir setting: it tests a one-stir result followed by a code lookup. The failure of depth 2 applies to the tested no-CoT rendering and strict gate, not an unqualified incapacity of Qwen to solve the family.

The corrected probe count is supported directly by `probes.json`: 110 of 112 total sites pass; restricted to q11, q12, and three emit cells, 33 of 35 pass; the two failures are `emit_colon|L56` and `emit_colon|L60`. The 77 earlier sites precede the differing question token, so they cannot demonstrate task invariance. High pre-divergence decodability is compatible with information carried forward from a common prefix. It does not establish computation performed for either later question.

The canonical 009 report still has stronger phrases beside its correction: §4 says “the model computes the hidden colour whether or not the answer needs it,” and §6 calls this a clean “computed but not needed” condition. Preferred wording: **z is probe-decodable under both questions, including before the questions diverge; the start-code task does not require it.** The report's own qualification acknowledges this issue but does not fully remove the stronger wording.

The bank remains potentially useful for model-side task-selectivity questions even though it failed the planned reader gate. Moving back to the old brew bank is a reasonable prioritization decision, not a logical requirement imposed by these results. I would not start the 009 reader phase merely to obtain another positive D: that would not close the central uncertainty.

### The unit and uncertainty are handled sensibly

The main point estimator is a correlation across pairs, so tables contributing more eligible partners receive more weight. Its confidence interval resamples whole tables and retains all associated pairs; pairs are not incorrectly bootstrapped as independent. Equal-table correlations are also saved. For Oracle–J_CB, pair r=0.634 versus table-mean r=0.548; the corresponding table-level CI is much wider [0.232,0.742]. Do not silently switch between pair and table estimands.

### A small independent confound check from existing outputs

I asked whether Oracle–J agreement could be explained entirely by some tables being generally easier. For each instrument, subtract its mean D within each table, then correlate the remaining partner-to-partner variation. This removes a table-level additive effect; it does not remove colour identity, within-table partner difficulty, or shared geometry.

### Wording that still outruns the result

1. **“The model's own output does not explain what the mid-layer readers share.”** The actual test removes only a linear term in one scalar output D_z. A low correlation or unchanged partial r rules out simple linear copying of that statistic, not nonlinear functions of the full colour-logit vector, other output tokens, or generic verbalizability. In particular, 006b's preplanned low-output-signal stratum had only nine pairs, below its minimum of fifteen. The decisive support condition was never met. Use: **the shared variation is not accounted for by a linear adjustment for the measured output D_z.**
2. **“The NLA is the less reliable instrument.”** Correlation between two deliberately different prefixes measures prefix robustness, not classical test–retest reliability. The p1 prefix forces colour, so it may change the construct being elicited. Dividing reader agreement by the square root of these prefix correlations is not a justified measurement-error correction without additional assumptions.
3. **“Scorer noise attenuates every r.”** Classical independent zero-mean measurement error would attenuate correlation; batch-layout errors can be systematic and shared. Their sign and magnitude cannot be inferred from the mere existence of bf16 sensitivity. The fixed-layout scorer is deterministic.
4. **“Reader D_z has no relation to single-cell leverage.”** Canonical saved CIs are Oracle L40 r=0.01584 [−0.20165,0.19760], Oracle L44 r=0.01462 [−0.32441,0.26220], and NLA r=−0.07616 [−0.37095,0.24912]. There are 82 gated pairs; all have positive colon S, with a restricted range. This is no detected pairwise linear association, not a powered equivalence/null result or a demonstration that the reader cannot discriminate causal from noncausal cases.
5. **“Neither reader follows leverage” across cells.** The Oracle's higher D at low-leverage `</think>` is a useful observed mismatch. However, a whole-cell patch changes every feature jointly, and a low effect can reflect redundancy, cancellation or an alternative route. It is not a content-specific necessity label. Across-pair S is also a normalized answer outcome, whereas D_z measures colour discrimination; proportionality is not a theorem even for a reader that accurately recovers causally relevant information.

The Oracle-only high-J correction is important: NLA J correlations are approximately 0.33–0.37, not 0.63–0.80. Report 29 now says this correctly; report 31's earlier summary still says readers collectively follow J.

## Most informative remaining checks using existing artifacts

1. **Does a reader provide incremental prediction of a defined causal measurement?** Join 010 per-pair D, 001 paired margins/G/S, and available model-side 007/008 outcomes at their actual matched sites/objects. Use table-held-out prediction and simple predeclared models. Compare reader-only, output-only, J-only, prompt metadata, and combined models. Report both Δm and S so denominator variation does not silently define the outcome. A reliable gain over baselines would support pragmatic causal-outcome prediction in scope; no gain with a wide interval remains inconclusive. None of this turns whole-cell S into z-specific causal truth. Artifacts: `results/010_reader_agreement/analysis/per_pair_D.json`, `outputs/001_leverage_profile/run/profile_brew.jsonl`, and matched 007/008 pair files identified by the main audit.
2. **How much Oracle–J association is lexical/contrast geometry rather than sample-specific content?** We have now ruled out a pure additive table effect. Next, use colour-role metadata in stage0 roles and 002/006 matrices to separate repeated colour-pair patterns from within-colour-pair variation, with table-level validation; avoid fitting a high-dimensional fixed-effects model with no effective replication. If Oracle–J association survives colour identities and multi-score output baselines, the case for shared activation-dependent geometry strengthens. It still does not establish use.
3. **Why does the Oracle double its z signal at `</think>`?** Existing whole/J/rest/random likelihoods and natural-colour positive controls can determine whether amplification is accompanied by broad colour discrimination, altered colour priors, or decomposition-dependent selectivity. Compare the full 12-colour score matrices and randomly selected qualitative readouts, not just D_z. A cell-independent fixed colour prior cancels in D, so that simplest explanation is already excluded; cell/input-dependent gain or interaction remains viable. Exact sources include `outputs/006_reader_likelihood/run1/ll_oracle_L{40,44}_p{0,1}.npz` and `outputs/007_jsubspace/stage1b/r1/ll/` (use on-contract layers).

These should be prioritized according to the main project's binding uncertainty. They require no new GPU runs. A future experiment intended to establish z-specific use needs an intervention and controls aimed at the content rather than merely at a correlated whole cell. That is a specification for Claude Code and the PI, not something this reviewer has implemented.
