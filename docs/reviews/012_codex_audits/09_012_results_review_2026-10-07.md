## 1. What was done and what is now secure

The actual overnight experiment is narrower than the original full design. It primarily swaps coordinates in pairs of J-token directions, at every prompt position over a band. Readers inspect clean and intervened states downstream. These are J-derived intervention outcomes, not reader-independent semantic labels. The class-mean/privilege split, multi-token study, natural-target reference program and AR route have not all been completed merely because the reader stages ran.

Stage A's clean-correct, clean-target-absent cohort contains 82 of 96 ST multihop items; nine lack token-direction coverage and five are clean incorrect.

| Intervention | Target-answer successes | Random-target control |
|---|---:|---:|
| J rows L24–47 | 48/82 | 5/82 |
| J rows L24–35 | 38/82 | 1/82 |
| J rows L40–48 | 45/82 | 2/82 |
| Logit rows L24–47 | 0/82 | — |

The target-answer endpoint is an accepted phrase in a short greedy continuation, not necessarily exact whole-response equality. Full-sequence float32-scored answer-margin changes corroborate a substantial effect. Stage B reproduces Stage A clean/J/random continuations exactly on all 87 covered items at both main bands. Zero-alpha edits preserve clean scores exactly; identity-clamp arithmetic can move margins by up to about 0.145 nats. The recorded multi-nat effects are much larger than that observation, although deterministic identity checks are not a universal numerical-error bound.

The four-item smoke was unrepresentative of the full bank. Its unchanged answers did not justify a geometric no-op explanation; the full results now establish intervention leverage directly.

The primary shorter-band effect is concentrated by target: Japan-target items succeed 26/27, other targets 12/55. At L24–47 the split is 27/27 versus21/55. This is not an artifact that erases the lever; it limits broad claims about arbitrary concepts. The post hoc reader cohort further restricts to the 64 items classified toward-target by clean read-position coordinates (36 successes). Its Japan subset has24/25 successes.

The reflection operator is not guaranteed to push toward the named replacement. Its sign depends on clean coordinates. The observed relationship between sign and success is useful, but a sign averaged at the read position is not a full account of the many-position intervention. “Explains a third of failures” should mean a descriptive association, not an identified causal fraction.

## 2. F1 is the strongest new result

At L24–35, masks vary the positions directly edited:

| Mask | Successful outcomes | Median answer-margin change |
|---|---:|---:|
| Every position | 38/82 | 5.04545 nats |
| Every position except the read token | 35/82 | 4.41729 nats |
| Read token only | 0/82 | 0.13903 nats |

All 35 all-but-read successes also occur in the all-position arm. Random controls produce1/82,1/82,0/82 successes. Saved mask checks and exact baseline parity support the manipulation as specified.

This establishes that direct writing at the final/read token is unnecessary for most observed flips under this intervention, and that writing only there is insufficient at this dose and band. After upstream edits, the model produces changed downstream states at the untouched read token; readers track those states.

It does **not** establish that the read token is unnecessary for computation. It still receives upstream information and remains on the answer-producing path. Nor does it identify the changed bridge as mediator rather than a propagated association or answer-related signal. The distinction is between direct writing and downstream propagation, not between artificial and natural information.

Reader outcome AUCs at the unwritten cell remain high, approximately .94–.96 for Oracle/NLA. The model's own final-output shifts are also highly predictive (.97082 bridge, .96832 answer). These observations rule out a simple “the reader sees only the coordinate we directly wrote into its cell” explanation; they do not remove output-facing accessibility.

## 3. F2's early NLA signal has a consequential position-selection error

### Diagnosis

Spec §13 excludes the position immediately preceding the read token when the prompt ends in ` is`. The saved model rows contain `is_read_minus_1` and `n_prompt`; the reader rows contain neither. In `scripts/012_reader_concordance/stageF_analysis.py`, the same `keep()` function checks the absent flag. It excludes the position from model-output averages but silently retains it in reader averages.

This affects52/82 clean-correct items, including40/64 toward-target items. The ledger's “cosmetic” missing-position metadata observation missed this additional substantive effect.

Confidence in the diagnosis is high. Root independently implemented the intended rule directly from Stage B `read_pos`/`read_token` and each raw row's `pos`, and reproduced both published and corrected point estimates exactly. AUC uses64 items; partial correlation uses63 because one has no answer-token contrast.

### Consequences

| NLA L42 analysis | Reader AUC | Model bridge-output AUC | Partial rho given output and size |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Saved, mismatched positions | .72023810 | .55257937 | .53596414 |
| Design exclusion applied to both | .58531746 | .55257937 | .17421558 |
| Predecessor retained for both, post hoc | .72023810 | .74007937 | .16479856 |

Using the intended common exclusion, the NLA's relation-cluster95% intervals are AUC[.44193,.76764] and partial rho[−.16519,.39237]. Retaining the predecessor for both gives partial interval[−.25278,.41401]. Neither aligned comparison supports the claimed positive association beyond the measured output variables.

The omitted predecessor alone has model bridge-output AUC1.0 in the40-item subgroup, compared with NLA .90598 and Oracle L44 .88604. This is a post hoc diagnostic, not independent validation, but it pinpoints the asymmetry.

Corrected Oracle L40/L44 AUCs are .39980/.47421. A paired NLA-minus-Oracle L44 AUC difference is .11111 with95% interval[−.03175,.18065]. A positive NLA-minus-L40 contrast exists, but the comparator is below chance and NLA does not meet the original positive-signal criterion. It does not establish a special NLA bridge-reading capability. Excluding Japan gives corrected NLA .62654 versus Oracle44 .59259 on39 items with12 successes.

The conclusion is **not** that NLA lacks early information. It is that the purported evidence for the highlighted early-position advantage does not survive the prespecified analysis, and the apparent beyond-output component depended on an unequal comparator.

### What Claude should change

Join reader rows to canonical position metadata by `(item,pos)`, assert identical included positions for readers and model baselines, and recompute the canonical CPU summaries/plots. No new GPU inference is needed. Record an amendment/correction and update the ledger's NLA-specific interpretation. If an all-early-position analysis is retained, label it post hoc and use the matching all-position baseline.

Codex has not applied this change. Root scratch point verification is in `/tmp/codex_012_rootchecks.json`; the Stage F report records independent bootstrap details and reproducible input paths.

### Implication for Stage R

A four-item broadcast smoke is saved and passes its runtime gates. That does not settle the research question. I recommend correcting F2 before scaling R as a replication of the .72/.54 finding.

Broadcast has explicit source concepts and was already inspected model-side in Stage A; it is a different-family extension with fresh reader measurements, not an untouched same-task confirmation. The proposed criterion “NLA passes and others fail” is also insufficient for specificity: use a paired reader difference. A confidence interval crossing zero in another reader does not establish that NLA differs from it.

On the same64 toward-target items:

| NLA L42 | .94544 |
| J_NP L40 / J_CB L40 | .91865 / .92460 |
| Logit lens L40 | .90179 |

| Model final-output bridge shift | .96925 |

The raw-derived AUCs reproduce. Oracle44–NLA Spearman of likelihood changes is .89762. This is compatible with strong shared response to the intervention; it is not the same statistic as 010's clean-state agreement and does not contradict010's weaker result. Correlation remains substantial within successes and within failures.

The simple ensemble shows no demonstrated predictive benefit. An independent paired relation-cluster interval for output-plus-ensemble minus output is[−.02273,+.02099]. Do not say all ensembles add nothing, or that there is exactly one shared signal. The chosen best reader was selected on the same data, the analysis is post hoc, and the output baseline is already near ceiling.

Excluding Japan leaves39 items and12 successes. Oracle44 AUC .89506, NLA .87037 and output .93519 remain substantial. The pooled near-perfect values partly reflect entity composition.

The final-output baseline is itself downstream of the intervention and closely related to the answer criterion. High output AUC is an important alternative explanation for apparent reader quality, not independent mechanistic evidence. Positive partial correlations beyond a few ranked scalar covariates do not quantify how much tracking is output-derived or remove every output-facing explanation.

### Naming and “failure” need narrower interpretations

The headline “Oracle-only7, NLA-only0” pools two Oracle layers with two samples each against one NLA layer with two samples. Equal-opportunity comparisons show:
- Oracle40-only7 versus NLA42-only2;
- Oracle44-only6 versus NLA42-only1.

There is a descriptive Oracle naming advantage under this word-match assay, but no basis for claiming NLA finds nothing Oracle misses. English exact-name matching also misses some adjectival/multilingual references and is not exhaustive semantic coverage.

All14 Oracle L40 “failed swaps naming the target” have positive answer-margin changes, from .11520 to5.47123 nats. They failed to produce the target greedy continuation; they did not lack all influence. Calling their reader responses false positives or “just detecting the edit” ignores these subthreshold effects.

Root inspected six seeded, unstratified items (seed20261007). Some readouts name target answers without naming target bridges; some retain both source and replacement associations; NLA often includes substantial task-format prose; Oracle sometimes invents contradictory factual context. For example, the Norway→Sweden intervention leaves the subject answering Oslo, while Oracle mentions both Oslo and Stockholm/Sweden. This is compatible with a partially changed state and an unreliable free-text explanation. A correct target mention alone does not validate the rest of the description.

## 5. Whitening and brew follow-ups

### G: use the raw recipe for the current assay

Whitened J swaps succeed26/82 versus38/82 raw. Every whitened success is a raw success:12 raw-only,0 whitened-only. Independent relation-cluster paired difference is−.14634, interval[−.23377,−.05882].

This supports poorer performance of the tested whitened recipe. It does not isolate covariance geometry: corpus centering, transformed coordinate normalization and realized dose change together. Read-token edits are larger after whitening (median maximal relative displacement .0792 versus .0304 raw). The white random control is larger still (.2265), so it is not a matched-dose null for answer-margin specificity.

No need to optimize whitening unless the cause of this loss becomes important for a decision. The raw lever already works.

### H: family is not an identified explanation

Brew's all-position atom intervention yields:

| Arm | Median S | 95% table-cluster interval |
|---|---:|---|
| Answer atoms L40–48 | .07274 | [.05601,.08875] |
| Answer atoms L24–47 | .15455 | [.11513,.23113] |
| Intermediate atoms L40–48 | .00500 | [.00200,.01151] |
| Intermediate atoms L24–47 | .01904 | [.00403,.02775] |

None flips a greedy answer. Late-band answer control remains below .15, while the wider/earlier band's point estimate just crosses that threshold. Its intermediate effect remains small, but the control gate is band-specific and uncertainty spans the threshold.

The ledger's “positions ruled out / family explains it” is not warranted. Relative to008, H also changes final-norm-folded versus raw J rows and SEC versus stock-bfloat16 computation. The vectors are similar but not identical; their difference is not demonstrated to cause the effect. H did not include a matched three-position condition under its own implementation. Even a pure increase from .010 to .073 would permit a partial positional contribution.

The supported conclusion is that the tested all-position late-band atom recipe remains too weak on brew to interpret its intermediate null. Why multihop differs remains unresolved. Colon-only dose logs also cannot establish that edits are small across every prompt position or that weak concept loading is the explanation.

## 6. Research directions, ranked

**Immediate, highest value: correct the F2 CPU analysis and the resulting interpretation.** Existing saved data settle this issue. Update Q's equal-opportunity naming and distinguish non-flips from zero effects at the same time. These corrections do not require new model runs. Do not launch a replication because an invalid comparison looked promising.

**Next substantive experiment: a small causal reference independent of J, aligned with what readers observe.** A natural source/target prompt pair plus whole-cell transfer at the actual read site could test whether that cell's change has answer influence without selecting directions through a reader. A clean-state rescue can complement it, with the usual redundancy caveat. Alternatively, a small class-mean pilot is justified if natural matching is impractical, but it needs its own leverage check and still does not automatically isolate bridge semantics. Start with a limited set of well-matched, target-balanced concepts; do not first generate the entire cue-varied bank. The result would determine whether concordance survives beyond a J-defined intervention family and, for a cell-transfer design, whether the measurement site carries demonstrable influence.

**If the objective is bridge specificity, vary the downstream relation while holding the replacement concept fixed.** Different relation-appropriate answers weaken simple fixed-answer steering. Include direct answer-direction controls and inspect natural-target sensitivity. Existing broadcast results motivate this route but their explicit source, repeated targets and prior exposure limit confirmatory claims. A balanced small pilot with distinct predicted answers is more informative than another ensemble sweep on the saturated final-token endpoint.

Deprioritize for now: more whitening tuning, a full MT expansion, AR phrase-vector work before the loader/loss contract is resolved, and a Stage R replication framed around the uncorrected F2 NLA advantage. A targeted same-convention brew mask comparison is optional if explaining008 matters; it is not required to continue the better-powered multihop reader study.

These are proposals for Claude Code and the PI, not actions taken by this auditor. No new research decision has been recorded as accepted.

## 7. Working conclusion and provenance limits

Established under the explored Qwen multihop conditions: a J-derived intervention can strongly shift answers; most primary flips persist without directly writing the read cell; readers track those propagated changes; their candidate-likelihood changes agree strongly; the tested rank ensemble offers no demonstrated gain beyond strong single/output baselines.

Unresolved: natural use of the bridge, a reader-independent per-cell semantic reference, a distinctive NLA signal beyond output-accessible information, the cause of the brew contrast, and broad generalization across targets/tasks.

This review preserves all earlier evidence distinctions: steering is not natural use, reader agreement is not truth, absent flips are not absent influence, and “outside J” is not “outside a functional workspace.” The spent-data ledger still applies. No confirmatory007c NLA rerun is recommended.
