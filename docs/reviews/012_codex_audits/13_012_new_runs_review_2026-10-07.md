## Assessment

The new experiments reinforce a limited but useful observation: several readers track interventions that change the subject's answer. They do not establish additional NLA-specific access to behaviorally relevant information beyond the measured output signal. Stage R inherits the consequential F2 position-filter error; correcting it leaves R's negative replication verdict intact and removes its positive residual-correlation point estimates.

E0/E0a make the local reconstructor and a centered J-transform interpretation of J-FVE more plausible. They do not pass the declared instrument-validation or metric-identification gates. Nor do they establish the proposed chain from J-FVE evaluation to active AR loss to Oracle reward to an explanation of 007.

The immediate next action should be Claude's correction of the existing CPU analyses and dependent claims. More GPU work is not required to resolve those errors.

## 1. What Stage R did and observed

R applies J-row source/target swaps across prompt positions at L24–35 on Jan's 192 single-token broadcast items. Unlike multihop, the source concept is explicit in the prompt. Readers assess changes in source-versus-target scores at positions before the final read token: NLA L42, Oracle L40/L44 and token lenses. The analysis averages these changes per item, then tests discrimination of successful answer swaps and correlation with the continuous answer-margin change.

There are 147 clean-correct items; one already contains the target answer, leaving 146 primary items. J swaps succeed on **39/146 (26.7%)**, versus **0/146** for the tested random-target control. The primary median margin changes are 1.3131483885 and 0.1119700832 nats, respectively. The reader comparison restricts to 109 toward-target items, with 32 successes and only 16 distinct source-concept clusters.

This establishes answer influence for this intervention relative to this control. It does not isolate a natural hidden-bridge mechanism. In particular, every prompt position is edited, and different positions can have different source/target coordinate orderings. Stratification by read-position direction does not describe every write.

For the literal §16 endpoint—all positions before the read token, identically for reader and output baseline—the AUCs are:

| Score | AUC |
|---|---:|
| NLA L42 | 0.9468344156 |
| Oracle L44 | 0.9472402597 |
| Oracle L40 | 0.9139610390 |
| Model's early-position bridge-output statistic | 0.9626623377 |
| Model's early-position answer-output statistic | 0.9358766234 |

AUC asks whether the score ranks successful interventions above unsuccessful ones. It is not the fraction of concepts faithfully represented or causally used. The model baseline also predicts success well. At these early positions it is a final-layer next-token distribution over a prompt prefix, not the final answer-position distribution; nonetheless it demonstrates substantial output-facing accessibility.

NLA minus Oracle L44 AUC is −0.0004058442, with paired source-cluster 95% interval [−0.0191390254, 0.0197368421]. NLA minus model bridge-output AUC is −0.0158279221 [−0.0648481765, 0.0119057409]. These post hoc paired comparisons provide no demonstrated NLA advantage over either. They do not prove universal equivalence or complete redundancy.

## 2. Stage R copied F2's mask error

`stageR_analysis.py` excludes the predecessor of final `is` using an `is_read_minus_1` field. Model rows contain this field; reader rows do not. Consequently, the model average discards a position which the reader average retains. This affects 33/146 primary items and 23/109 toward-target items.

Root independently read the code and raw schemas, then reimplemented AUC by direct positive/negative comparisons and partial Spearman with SciPy ranks and least-squares residualization. The original and both aligned-mask point statistics reproduce exactly. No project analysis was run or changed.

| Endpoint | NLA partial rho | Oracle L44 partial rho |
|---|---:|---:|
| Published, mismatched positions | 0.3441045214 | 0.4023550634 |
| All early positions for both | **−0.0643491055** | **0.1187179166** |
| Exclude pre-`is` position for both | **−0.0574430398** | **0.0735301149** |

For the all-early endpoint, the source-cluster 95% intervals are [−0.3314121392, 0.1614803978] and [−0.2628674886, 0.3859361969]. These partials control for the model's bridge and answer scores and the recorded read-position edit-size summary. They are residual associations, not causal mediation estimates or a comprehensive removal of output-facing explanations. The canonical 2,000-draw bootstrap is reproduced in report 14; direct reader-difference intervals use 10,000 draws.

§16 says all positions before the read token but also describes its protocol as identical to F2. The latter had a special predecessor exclusion. Both coherent readings should therefore be disclosed; neither preserves positive NLA residual evidence. Under the common exclusion, both NLA and Oracle L44 AUC equal 0.9504870130.

**High-confidence diagnosis:** the mask mismatch is real and affects the scientific comparison. Claude should join explicit positional metadata into both sources, apply one declared inclusion set, assert identical `(item, pos)` sets, and recompute the F/R summaries and derivative prose.

The canonical F2 positive headline also remains uncorrected at this snapshot. Its highlighted NLA AUC 0.720238 and partial 0.535964 become 0.585317 and 0.174216 under its intended shared exclusion; the latter interval includes zero. It is therefore misleading to describe the original positive hint solely as awaiting fresh hidden-bridge replication. The existing analysis error undermines the evidence for that hint. R's changed family is a legitimate limitation, but it does not rescue F2's mismatched comparison.

R is new reader measurement on a bank previously examined model-side in Stage A. It is not wholly untouched confirmation. Its NLA-specificity rule also needs a paired reader difference: one reader passing a significance threshold while another fails is insufficient.

## 3. E0: useful calibration evidence, failed validation gate

E0 runs the accepted local 011 loader conventions across 12 tagged layers on 1,024 sampled spans with lengths 1–32 tokens. It compares reconstruction metrics with saved published metadata. Root independently recomputed the gate counts from full-precision JSON:

| Check | Layers meeting tolerance | Rule |
|---|---:|---:|
| Scaled whitened FVE | 12/12 | at least 10/12 |
| Retrieval@1 | **8/12** | at least 10/12 |
| Unscaled whitened FVE | 2/12 | at least 10/12 for full validation |

The declared verdict, **not validated even up to scale**, is correct. Retrieval misses are at L20–32. Mid-layer agreement is encouraging: L40 scaled FVE 0.233459 versus published 0.224, retrieval 0.900794 versus 0.92; L44 0.212609 versus 0.2202 and 0.913690 versus 0.93. Failure of the acceptance gate does not diagnose whether the remaining mismatch is in the loader or evaluation distribution/protocol.

Three qualifications materially limit the interpretation:

1. **The scales are fitted separately per layer.** There are 12 fitted scalars, ranging from 0.1127607423 to 2.3344610489. This is not one global calibration across the model. Fitting occurs on the same samples subsequently scored.
2. **Scaling is about the whitener mean.** With T = W(y−μ) and P = W(ŷ−μ), alpha = <P,T>/<P,P>. The resulting FVE is the squared cosine between the flattened arrays. In raw coordinates, the adjusted prediction is μ + alpha(ŷ−μ). It demonstrates aggregate directional alignment after amplitude fitting, not correct native output scale or reproduction of the published scoring formula.
3. **The text contexts are reused.** The spans are recrops of the same 12 deterministic responses used to select 011's loader conventions. Audit 15 reconstructed all 768 earlier saved crops from these responses. There are 792 distinct preceding-token target positions among 1,024 crops; retrieval evaluates 1,008 rows in 42 pools of 24, dropping the remainder. This is an expanded diagnostic on reused calibration contexts, not fresh held-out validation of the locally selected convention.

The depth-dependent calibration is consistent with a scale-invariant loss. It does not imply that loss, nor that published FVEs are scale-aligned. Missing output transforms, normalization and different metric conventions remain alternatives. A common positive scale cancels from normalized within-layer concept differences, so scale alone does not invalidate AR interventions; directional and input-dependent errors remain the relevant uncertainty.

## 4. E0a: a leading J-FVE candidate, not a recovered training objective

After E0, candidate metrics were rescored with per-layer least-squares amplitude fitting. The best candidate compares **J_L(y−μ_L)** with **J_L(ŷ−μ_L)**. It matches J-FVE within 0.04 at **8/11** layers and J-retrieval within 0.07 at **10/11**. The identification rule requires at least 9/11 for each, so the metric remains open. All L32–60 FVE comparisons pass; L20–28 fail.

This full J-transform metric is distinct from 007's dense orthogonal rank-1,024 projector and the sparse J components in 008. Similar names do not make their geometries interchangeable.

The other candidates receive separately fitted target/prediction centering before amplitude fitting, while reusing retrieval scores from their original uncentered vectors. Positive scaling preserves cosine retrieval; this centering does not. FVE and retrieval can be complementary metrics, but they do not assess an identical transformed-vector convention in those candidates. The best candidate uses a shared external mean and is not directly affected by that mismatch. The dense candidate has only three layers and cannot meet a nine-layer threshold by construction.

Metadata gives substantive evidence for a J-aware AR training/evaluation setup. The stronger chain remains unverified:

- approximate recovery of a validation metric does not show that it supplied training gradients;
- an active AR J loss does not show that the Oracle reward explicitly includes that loss;
- “joint bullet-FVE” need not mean joint raw-plus-J FVE;
- even a confirmed J objective would not by itself explain the magnitude or geometry of 007's reader dependence.

The ledger's sentence “The AR was then trained with a J-lens-transformed reconstruction term” outruns the metric comparison. Keep it as a metadata-supported hypothesis pending the actual loss definition. An indirect bias through the learned AR is also different from an explicitly J-weighted Oracle reward.

## 5. Recommended decisions

**First: correct the existing F/R analyses and claims.** No new measurements are needed. This directly changes the basis for prioritizing another NLA replication. My confidence in the diagnosis is high.

**Next scientific question: does reader agreement predict answer-relevant changes under an independent intervention family?** Current labels are generated with J rows, so shared sensitivity to J-derived edits remains an alternative. A small target-balanced natural source/target transfer or class-mean pilot, with its own leverage and specificity checks, would be more discriminative than further reader-ensemble tuning. Relation-dependent answers under the same proposed concept change would help separate bridge substitution from generic answer attraction. Proposed work belongs to Claude and still requires the PI's decision; it was not executed here.

**AR question: what are the official forward, evaluation, training-loss and reward contracts?** Obtain the exact definitions separately, ideally with one official input/output fixture. That is more informative than searching nearby scoring variants until a threshold passes. If independent local validation remains needed, freeze the convention, calibrate on one response-level text set and evaluate on genuinely new response texts. New calibration alone cannot recover the historical objective.

The strong prior F1 observation remains: 35/82 multihop swaps work without writing the read cell directly, versus 38/82 with all positions edited and 0/82 at the read cell alone. That supports downstream propagation of intervention effects. Neither the new R correlations nor E0's metric profile upgrades it to a complete concept-specific mechanism.
