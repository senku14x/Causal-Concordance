# Independent audit of 012 Stages A, G and H — 2026-10-07

Read-only review of `docs/ledger/012_ledger.md`, design §§11–15, saved A/G/H and Stage B comparison rows, and the narrow intervention/scoring paths required to interpret their comparisons. No GPU experiments, canonical workflow runs, repository changes or dependency changes. CPU recomputations used saved JSON and small geometry tensors only. No private correspondence is reproduced here.

## Main conclusions

1. **Stage A establishes an effective J-row intervention on this selected multihop bank.** The original four-item smoke was unrepresentative. The full run gives 48/82 target-answer successes at L24–47 and 38/82 at L24–35; matched source-preserving random-target controls give 5/82 and 1/82. This is evidence of intervention influence, not a semantic mediation label. The primary shorter-band result is strongly concentrated in Japan-target items.
2. **Stage G's tested whitened recipe performs worse than the raw recipe on target-answer success.** All 26 whitened successes were also raw successes; 12 raw successes disappear and none appear. This is not a general finding that whitening is bad: centering, the coordinate geometry, directions and realized intervention size change together.
3. **Stage H does not warrant “family explains it” or “positions ruled out.”** Every-position L40–48 editing remains below the answer-control power threshold, but the result is not a position-only comparison against 008, and L24–47 has a stronger answer control that just crosses the point-estimate threshold. The sound conclusion is that this all-position late-band recipe is still a weak handle on brew. Why remains unresolved.

However, 26/38 successes in the reader-compatible primary band have Japan as target. Excluding Japan, success is 12/55 = 0.21818, below the 0.30 absolute rate used in the original aggregate lever gate. Source ablation disrupts 23/82 original answers while random ablation disrupts none; disruption is not evidence that the bridge's semantic content was specifically removed. Logit-row swaps showing zero successes is a useful alternative-direction comparison, not proof that J accesses the original natural mechanism.

### What changed besides the word “whitening”

The `WhiteSwap` class in `stageG_capture.py` transforms unit raw J covectors by the inverse whitener, renormalizes them, centers activations by the corpus mean, clamps whitened clean coordinates, and maps the displacement back. The raw `SwapEdit` has no corpus centering. This is a legitimate, explicitly defined intervention package, but the comparison does not isolate covariance geometry from centering or dose.

Independent dose summaries at the **read position only**:

| Arm | Median maximum layerwise norm ratio ||Δh||/||h|| | Median sum of layerwise ||Δh|| | Median Δm |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Raw J | 0.0304404850 | 6.3878044980 | 5.0454477351 |
| Whitened J | 0.0792193608 | 17.9243530408 | 3.6679735817 |
| Raw random target | 0.0722534609 | 34.4707168341 | 0.2778318450 |
| Whitened random target | 0.2265354870 | 98.4466208220 | 2.8690029502 |

Whitening does not simply weaken the raw Euclidean displacement. It gives larger recorded edits and fewer flips. Stronger dose could still be less useful or more disruptive; a monotonic response cannot be assumed. The source-preserving random control is also much larger than the intended intervention and creates a substantial positive margin shift. Its 3/82 target successes remain low, but it is not a matched-dose null for interpreting Δm specificity.

The saved G gates support a correctly instantiated coordinate operation: no collisions, no G0 failures, exact clean parity with B, and float64 RA error below 1.6e−14. Whitened FP32 coordinate errors at the read position are at most 2.15e−6; the bf16 post-cast coordinate error reaches 0.02865. Raw equivalents are about 4.05e−6 and 0.06680. These are not on their own measurement-error guarantees for answer margins.

The sigma artifact records **100 sampled conversations**, 50,199 tokens, and Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage approximately 0.0033–0.0053. The ledger's “128 conversations” is stale/inaccurate. Float64 round-trip errors are approximately 2.4e−14 or less. These algebraic checks do not validate the choice of corpus metric as optimal for this task.

The observed G reader pattern should remain scoped to whitened interventions and the selected toward-target cohort. High AUC is not an independent resolution of the output-facing confound merely because the intervention metric changed.

The late-band answer control remains below 0.15, so the late-band z effect is not an informative mechanistic null under the rule. If discussed, this latter comparison should be explicitly exploratory and band-specific; the written verdict rule singled out L40–48 and cannot convert the alternative band into a confirmatory claim.

The paper-style atom handle is weaker here than the earlier class-mean lever. That is a result about the chosen intervention, not an explanation of why brew differs.

### The comparison with 008 changes more than positions

1. **Direction construction:** 008 uses unit atoms `(gain ⊙ W_U[token]) @ J`, where gain is the final RMSNorm's effective gain (`trackb.py:131,157`; `jspace.py:21–45`). H uses Jan's raw `W_U[token] @ J` bank. The twelve lowercase leading-space token IDs are identical. I directly compared saved vectors: row cosine median 0.997046, minimum 0.980469; normalized pair-contrast cosine median 0.993316 for z pairs and 0.995569 for answer pairs, minima 0.961948 and 0.969124. These are similar but not identical interventions. The mismatch is not demonstrated to explain the result, but it prevents identifying position scope as the sole manipulated factor.
2. **Numerical regime:** 008 executes the edited late blocks through SEC's true-float32 replay. H uses stock bf16 blocks and residuals, with float32 output scoring. A zero-alpha pass checks baseline identity, not invariance between these numerical regimes.
3. **No matched three-position H arm:** H did not run the same raw directions and stock numerical regime on only 008's three cells. Therefore neither a position effect nor its absence is isolated.

Even if these implementation differences were negligible, an answer-control improvement from about 0.010 to 0.073 would support a partial effect of the expanded setup. Failing to cross an arbitrary power threshold does not logically prove that positions played no role. More broadly, “family” groups prompt format, reasoning task, answer margins, concept identities, loading and many other factors; it is a description of the comparison, not an identified mechanism.

The warranted wording is: **“Every-position J-row swaps at L40–48 remain insufficiently powerful on this brew cohort under the tested recipe. Expanding positions did not produce the strong control needed to interpret its z null; the reason for the multihop–brew difference remains unresolved.”**

### Normalization and dose caveats

H reuses 008's G, while recomputing its own clean gap as a diagnostic. The largest relative G difference is 2.6331%; median G from 008 is 9.58573 nats. Replacing G with H's own gap gives answer-control medians 0.0733693 (L40–48) and 0.156573 (L24–47), versus 0.0727415 and 0.154554. This does **not** account for the broad conclusion or threshold pattern. It is a small cross-regime calibration difference rather than the main flaw.

H logs displacement norms only at the colon. The median maximum ratio there is approximately 1–2%. It does not measure edits across the rest of the prompt, including explicit colour-table tokens. Consequently, the ledger's explanation “colour concepts load weakly on their J rows, so the swap barely edits anything” is not established for this all-position intervention. Source-target contrast, clean coordinates and dose at all edited positions would be required to diagnose that explanation. The original reflection identity also means source loading alone does not determine the edit: the source-versus-target contrast matters.

## What is worth doing next for these branches

No full rerun is needed to establish A's lever or G's paired loss. The substantive next questions depend on the intended claim:

- If the goal is simply a usable intervention for reader experiments, the raw multihop lever already exists. Do not optimize whitening merely because it is a more elaborate metric.
- If explaining the brew difference is important, ask Claude for a small matched comparison at L40–48: identical directions, tokenizer, numerical regime, clean-reference and scoring; vary only three-cell versus all-position mask. Record all-position dose and clean contrast signs. Otherwise leave the reason unresolved and correct the headline.
- If attributing G's loss specifically to whitening matters, the cheapest discriminating additions are raw-with-corpus-centering and a clearly defined dose comparison, rather than immediately changing covariance estimators. This is optional; it should be justified by a decision that depends on the cause of the loss.
- Keep the Japan/non-Japan split adjacent to claims of general multihop performance. A balanced fresh-target study would answer generalization, but is not needed to acknowledge the effects already observed.
