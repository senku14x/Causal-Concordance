## 1. The priors that do and do not support this design

**A multihop lever is plausible enough to test cheaply.** Jan's original causal figure supports approximately 59% J-row ST multihop swap success, with low random-control success. Broadcast is weaker, approximately 26%. These are visual estimates, not full-precision artifacts. The manuscript acknowledges tuned bands, unequal method denominators, repeated targets, permissive answer matching and limited generalization. The portable clone explicitly says CPU/toy tests do not establish Qwen numerical reproduction. Stage A tests a released reproduction path; its code is a reference implementation, not a gold standard established by the historical figure.

**The earlier negative brew result remains scoped.** Jan's successful multihop result and 008's weak atom swaps differ in family, concepts, rendered prompt, edit positions, layer band, numerical regime and success endpoint. Report 32's inference that 008 was very likely explained by band/positions is too strong. The proposed sweep can isolate band effects within multihop. It will not identify which difference explains brew versus multihop.

**Oracle > NLA swap-following is a hypothesis with weakly transferable support.** The Oracle/NLA distinction in 007 was brew at the colon. On multihop, Oracle L40 whole/J bridge D was about 14.7/12.8; NLA L42 was about 14.7/14.5. Both retained substantial signal in dense J. Reader-specific decomposition also varied with position in brew. Furthermore, sensitivity to a dense rank-1024 projection does not directly predict response to a two-row swap. Keeping Oracle > NLA as a prospective prediction is fine; describing it as the expected implication of 007 is not.

**The class-mean arm is helpful but not a guaranteed escape from the output confound.** It constructs directions without a J reader. It does not ensure those directions exclude J geometry, lexical content, the answer, or correlated cues. Jan's manuscript also reports that uncorrected mean differences did not reproduce the strong-ridge template result. That is a reason to test their lever early, not assume class means will work because they are simpler.

**The AR contract remains a separate uncertainty.** Keeping E blocked on authoritative loader/loss clarification is justified. Broadening colour words to phrases does not automatically validate the loader or align reconstructed predictive activations with intermediate reasoning. The brief CPU-only description must not be read as permission for unvalidated model inference; this is not a prerequisite for A–D.

## 2. What a successful swap would label

Stage B's valid label is an outcome of **item × exact prompt × arm × band × dose**: this intervention changed the designated answer by the stated criteria.

That is different from:
- the bridge was the mediator of the answer change;
- the captured downstream cell supplied the consequential information;
- the bridge is the sole correct concept for a reader to report;
- another reader's additional content is false or unused.

The intervention edits every prompt position over many layers. Stage C reads one downstream residual. Other positions and downstream cached states can carry the answer-changing effects. A strong global intervention can coexist with a weakly informative captured cell. Reading downstream removes literal immediate inspection of a just-written coordinate, but the residual path can preserve the injected component without using that particular component to produce the answer.

A J-derived manipulation is also not reader-independent merely because label code never calls a reader. Its construction and item selection favor that geometry. This is not a tautology—J edits need not work—but it prevents treating J-swap success as an independent gold standard for ranking J-based readers. Separate J-derived intervention diagnostics from labels constructed using full class means or natural-state patches. Call full class means **J-independent in construction**, not non-J.

For exploration, narrowing the claim is sufficient. The current design can study responsiveness and predictive association under answer-influencing interventions. If the desired claim becomes per-cell mediation, a small transfer of the edited read-cell state into a clean run, plus complementary clean-state rescue in an edited run, would add relevant evidence. Positive results help localize influence; negative results remain compatible with distributed effects. If the desired claim becomes bridge-role specificity, reuse an intermediate replacement across downstream relations predicting different answers. The existing broadcast material is relevant to this, although its repeated-target and prompt-quality limitations remain.

The sentence in §8 that a failed swap means “unused through that direction” must also be narrowed. The result is no detected or no qualifying answer effect under the specified intervention. Wrong direction, weak dose, redundant computation and downstream failure all remain possible.

## 3. Two Stage C interpretation rules should be removed

### Energy share is not semantic-content share

The claim that 84–94% of source content remains outside J converts vector energy or reconstruction variance into semantic information. There is no basis for that conversion. A small vector component may carry a decisive bit, a large remainder can be semantically unrelated, and information can be redundantly recoverable from both.

The cited fractions also concern other constructions and examples in the Workspace paper, not the two-row multihop swaps being run here. A low-rank edit at many positions and layers does not make a globally small or semantically minor intervention.

Therefore source naming after a successful swap is not the default of a faithful reader. It may reflect retained source content, cue-based reconstruction, reader insensitivity, an uninformative read location, or an answer change that bypasses the proposed bridge representation. Target naming also has several possible explanations. Both can coexist in an edited state.

### Equal naming after successful and failed edits is not proof of artifact reading

The injected content is part of the model state. A reader might correctly detect a changed component even when the answer remains unchanged, because that component is unused, redundant, or insufficient for later computation. Conversely, answer influence can travel elsewhere.

If target naming is equally strong after effective and ineffective swaps, the justified conclusion is that this response does not discriminate their answer outcomes in this sample. That is useful evidence about outcome specificity, without declaring the reader's description false.

Natural-target prompts provide a sensitivity reference: can the reader discriminate source from target on ordinary inputs? They do not define the unique correct interpretation of a hybrid edited state. A real-target prompt changes other lexical and semantic properties as well. Keep src-only / tgt-only / both / neither categories; avoid forcing source and target into mutually exclusive truth labels.

## 4. The smoke contains a real contamination issue and an incorrect geometric explanation

Saved rows show:
- atomic-26-symbol: clean, intervention and random all Fe;
- atomic-79-symbol: all Au;
- birthstone-emerald-month: all 5;
- chem-bones-Z: all 26, already the replacement answer in the clean run.

Thus there are zero new target-answer transitions, and zero target successes among the three clean-source-correct items. The unfiltered 1/4 is not evidence of a successful intervention. Excluding clean-incorrect rows from the primary rate is appropriate; also exclude clean-target-present outputs because the permissive matcher can accept both source and target in one continuation. Preserve all rows and distinguish eligibility from intervention failure.

The three unchanged completions are not evidence of activation no-ops. All four rows have zero collision-skipped layers. The geometric explanation in the execution note is false for this pseudoinverse construction.

For unit non-collinear source and target vectors s,t, at a clean activation h with alpha=1, set V=[s,t] and exchange c=V†h. Then

`delta_h = V(swap(c)-c) = -2 u(u^T h), u=(s-t)/||s-t||.`

This is reflection of the normalized contrast component. As s and t approach each other, the pseudoinverse coefficient difference compensates for the smaller vector difference. High cosine alone does not force the edit to vanish. Root derived this identity independently and checked it with CPU numerical examples at cosine .83, .93 and .99; errors were below 3e-16. Actual neural activations could still have a small contrast projection, but that must be measured.

At later layers, current h differs from the clean reference, so the general operation is `(I-VV†)h + V swap(V†h_clean)`, not simply reflection of current h. Existing artifacts save no applied-layer counts, edit norms, achieved coordinate errors or answer margins. They cannot distinguish small edits, large ineffective edits, small non-flipping probability shifts and runtime application problems.

The narrow hook path looks structurally correct; no actual hook bug was found. Recommend a small diagnostic wrapper/smoke for Claude, leaving the reference clone unchanged: record called layers, actual pre/post-cast edit magnitude, clamp residuals and source/target margins. An unchanged greedy answer should not be explained geometrically without those observations.

## 5. Rendering and parity need one explicit contract

Jan's path strips trailing whitespace before encoding. The spec proposes WSB-render captures for readers. Editing every position does not make those prompts equivalent: a changed suffix can change the last token, clean coordinates, answer boundary and behavior.

There is a useful nuance: if appending a space preserves token-prefix identity, earlier last-word activations in a causal model may be unchanged. That does not guarantee the subsequent space state or answer behavior is unchanged, and tokenization must be checked. Readers must receive captures from the exact labeled intervention run.

Stage A can reproduce Jan's stripped rendering. Stage B parity should match those exact token IDs and endpoints. A substantive WSB-render condition should receive its own clean gate and causal measurement; do not silently attach Stage A labels to 007 captures.

The reference generator uses accepted-answer length plus two tokens, capped at eight; smoke budgets were 3,3,4,5. It then scores source- and target-specific prefixes. Stage B says eight tokens. Its accepted-phrase matcher is substring-like at the word-sequence level, not exact whole-answer equality. Matching reference success for parity and evaluating strict scientific answer correctness are distinct tasks.

G-parity should therefore compare identical render, generation budget, accepted answers and parser. Report the paired success/failure table, not just 90% aggregate agreement: a mostly negative cohort can hide failures to reproduce the few successes. Same-layout repeated clean runs and zero edits establish determinism/identity; they do not by themselves measure systematic bfloat16 rounding sensitivity. Use actual post-cast coordinate fidelity and selected layout/regime comparisons when needed, rather than interpreting zero repeat variance as an arithmetic-error bound.

## 6. A minimal reader comparison that can answer something

Choose one working early intervention band and a fixed item cohort. Feed the same edited trajectories to Oracle at on-contract layers and NLA at L42.

The current Oracle L24–35 and NLA L24–39 routes would confound reader identity with intervention. If L24–35 works, it permits both. If only L24–39 works, Oracle L44 and NLA L42 are both downstream and available; Oracle L40 can be a near-band boundary diagnostic, but L44 gives a clearer separation. If only L24–47 works, do the late Oracle study and state that an on-contract downstream NLA comparison is unavailable.

The unavoidable different reader layers remain part of the instrument comparison; do not add avoidable different bands or success-selected item sets.

Read whole activations first:
- clean;
- targeted intervention;
- matched random intervention;
- natural-target reference.
Retain successes, partial effects and failures in a fixed cohort, or use a prespecified stratified sample with known weights. The current “each arm that works” state list contradicts its planned failed/random comparison. Save enough captures during the subject run to avoid an expensive reload just to recover omitted controls.

A useful primary reader statistic is the within-item change in target-versus-source preference:
`d_r = [LL_r(tgt|edited)-LL_r(src|edited)] - [LL_r(tgt|clean)-LL_r(src|clean)].`
Specify spellings and multi-token scoring. Inspect absolute source and target scores too. Natural-target versus clean contrasts calibrate each reader's sensitivity; avoid dividing by a weak or zero reference contrast. Raw nats from different verbalizers need not have identical calibration.

Compare these reader changes with continuous model margin change and strict answer outcome. Inspect whether model-margin movement is target promotion or source collapse, and whether selected answers are substantive completions rather than mere mentions. A source-preserving random-target control and actual-dose-matched random edit threaten different alternatives; neither makes all targeted effects bridge-specific.

Outcomes would be interpretable:
- real-target controls work, but reader scores do not move under effective interventions: a mismatch to diagnose between reader sensitivity, measurement cell and causal path;
- reader changes equally under effective and ineffective edits: intervention sensitivity without demonstrated outcome specificity;
- one reader tracks outcomes better on the same sample: scoped predictive advantage under this assay;
- both follow real-target and targeted states selectively: concordance on intervention-induced changes, still not proof of natural mechanism.

There is no need to begin with every piece × reader × handle × layer. Dense pieces can follow once this whole-state comparison contains interpretable variation.

## 7. Agreement, ensembles, sample size and controls

Q1/Q2 are legitimate descriptive questions. Q3/Q4 need an explicit finite endpoint before “accuracy,” “false positive” or “ensemble gain” is meaningful. Successful edits are not positive labels for every named concept; failed edits are not negative labels for concept existence. Random slices can retain genuine information. Prompt-only readers can name the bridge correctly without internal access.

If the target is predicting intervention success, name it that and evaluate held-out predictive utility when thresholds are tuned. If the target is source/target naming, define a common candidate set, aliases, top-k/thresholds for token lenses, output budgets for verbalizers and both/neither categories. Union recall otherwise grows mechanically with verbosity. Report marginal rates and positive agreement alongside kappa. Naive majority gives J_NP and J_CB two closely related votes; a family-balanced sensitivity is appropriate if ensemble conclusions depend on this.

Q5 can test recoverability outside a specified dense J projection. It cannot turn that complement into a definition of “outside the workspace.” It also cannot yet answer the SAE or skip-lens question when those instruments are absent.

Root independently reproduced the covered-bank counts from the saved unresolved-vocabulary artifact and frozen ST JSON: 87/96 items remain before clean gating; Japan is the target in 28/87; 47 distinct covered targets, 37 appearing once. Per-target tables are diagnostic, not precise estimates. Report pooled and no-Japan results, matched-item reader differences and relation-cluster uncertainty. Wilson intervals alone understate the dependence relevant to generalizing beyond the bank. Avoid a complex model whose apparent precision comes from treating correlated items as independent.

Fifty clean items and a 30% lever may yield only fifteen successes, before splitting bands/pieces/readers. This is not automatically adequate for all proposed contrasts. Keep continuous outcomes and a few primary comparisons. A full run can be useful exploration without being a powered null test.

Output rank/D and J loading are valuable alternative predictors. Post-edit output and loading are descendants of the intervention, not ordinary pre-existing confounders. Adjusted results should be called incremental prediction beyond these measured variables. A scalar partial correlation cannot eliminate the full output-facing explanation. A prompt-only baseline fixed on unchanged text is especially informative about within-prompt differences, but does not prevent a reader from reconstructing answers from cue information that survived internally.

Apply positive controls to the null interpretation they protect. Failure of answer-row steering does not erase robust positive bridge steering or veto a separately powered class-mean arm. The full class-mean effect is the leverage check for its J/remainder decomposition. Save continuous contrasts and random-control outcomes; the 1-nat-plus-flip rule is an operational bin, not a confidence statement about each item.

Class-mean prompt construction should vary cues and downstream relations. Leaving out the exact test item alone does not prevent shared cue/template or answer leakage. Inspect a small sample before scaling their generation/gating.
