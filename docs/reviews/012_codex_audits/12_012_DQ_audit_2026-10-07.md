# Independent audit of 012 Stage D output checks and Stage Q concordance

Date: 2026-10-07. All analyses are exploratory. Repository and canonical outputs were read only. This review used the current `docs/ledger/012_ledger.md`, `results/012_reader_concordance/analysis/stageD_answers.json`, `stageQ_concordance.json`, their narrow CPU analysis code, and the corresponding Stage B/C JSONL records. Independent calculations were performed in `/tmp/audit012_dq.py`, without importing or running project workflows.

## Main assessment

The saved data support strong concordance of readers' **changes in source-versus-target likelihood** after the same J-row swap, and an association between those changes and whether the continuation includes the target answer. The subject model's own output distribution predicts this outcome at least as well in point estimates. Thus good reader performance on this particular endpoint is insufficient evidence that a reader isolates an internal bridge independently of answer-facing information.

This is not evidence that readers all recover identical semantic content, that the output explains a known fraction of reader tracking, or that ensembling is generally useless. All these comparisons are on the same intervention dataset and are not independent confirmations of natural-use fidelity.

## Ensemble conclusion: no observed benefit, not proven equivalence

Stage Q appropriately gives Oracle, NLA, J-lens and logit families equal weight and averages ranks rather than raw nats. Its ensemble-versus-best interval compares against Oracle L44, selected as best on these same data. It does not account for that selection, and this is a post hoc ensemble comparison rather than a held-out benchmark.

An independent 10,000-draw relation-cluster bootstrap (27 clusters; seed 20261007; ranks held fixed as in Stage Q) gives:

| Paired AUC difference | 95% percentile interval |
|---|---|
| Ensemble minus Oracle L44 | [-0.04136076952332659, 0.009744057724957494] |
| Output-plus-ensemble minus output alone | [-0.022727272727272707, 0.020991375154994534] |
| Oracle L44 minus output alone | [-0.06617690515922679, 0.02314814814814814] |
| NLA L42 minus output alone | [-0.10369483723513323, 0.023245057587926346] |

The first interval agrees closely with the saved 2,000-draw interval. The added-reader point gain over output is only 0.001984126984126978, and the uncertainty spans both signs. Say **this simple rank ensemble shows no demonstrated predictive benefit on this cohort and endpoint**. “Ensembling adds nothing” and “the readers share one signal” exceed the evidence. Ceiling effects also leave only about 0.031 AUC available above the output baseline. Different endpoints, error costs or combinations remain untested.

Bootstrap uncertainty is conditional on the chosen cohort, intervention band, instruments and rank calibration. Relation clustering does not model every dependency from repeated target concepts.

## Naming coverage: an unequal opportunity comparison and an invalid negative interpretation

The reported “Oracle-only 7, NLA-only 0” pools two Oracle layers, each with two samples, against one NLA layer with two samples. Pooling gives Oracle four opportunities and NLA two. The per-layer, equal-sample comparisons on 36 successful swaps are:

| Comparison | Oracle-only | NLA-only | Neither |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Oracle L40 vs NLA L42 | 7 | 2 | 0 |
| Oracle L44 vs NLA L42 | 6 | 1 | 1 |

Oracle names the target on 34/36 successful items at either layer; NLA on 29/36. This is a real descriptive Oracle advantage in this exact word-matching assay, but “NLA finds nothing Oracle misses” hides the pooled-layer advantage. Naming uses exact English whole-word forms (plus specified aliases); multilingual and adjectival references can be missed, so it is not exhaustive semantic coverage.

There are 14 Oracle L40 items called “failed swaps naming target.” **Every one has a positive answer-margin change**, ranging from 0.11519703893953093 to 5.471225738525391 nats. Several approach five nats. Here failure means no qualifying greedy target continuation, not zero causal influence. These observations cannot be treated as false-positive content or as proof the reader detects only the write. They are compatible with real subthreshold answer changes. This distinction matters even though the categorical success endpoint itself is a valid descriptive label.

The coverage statistic “strong shift on failed” chooses its threshold as the median reader shift among successes. It is an outcome-calibrated descriptive threshold, not a independently validated false-positive rate. The other coverage statistic, no nonpositive reader shifts among successes, tests direction of a specified candidate contrast, not spontaneous discovery of unknown concepts.

## What the output comparison can and cannot establish

The model baseline reads its final-layer distribution at the same answer-producing position. It is downstream of the intervention and closely related to the response being predicted. Its high AUC is therefore an important alternative explanation for why the reader assay looks good, not independent evidence of a hidden mechanism. The answer score is first-token based with shared first-token alternatives dropped; the reader verbalizers can score complete candidate strings. These are useful comparisons but not identical measurements.

The modest positive partial correlations suggest that reader scores are not merely an exact linear function of the ranked scalar output covariates and edit size. They do not show independence from the entire output distribution, and cannot quantify what fraction of tracking is “shared.” Those output quantities and edit size are post-intervention measurements, so the residualization is an incremental-association diagnostic rather than a causal mediation decomposition. Multiple exploratory cells and broad intervals further limit claims that particular readers uniquely retain signal.

## Recommended interpretation and next use

Preserve the strong narrow result: after J-row swaps on these final-token multihop states, all measured readers' candidate likelihood changes predict the intervention outcome, and their rank ordering largely overlaps. The model's own output is already an excellent predictor. The initial Oracle-over-NLA hypothesis is not supported by a decisive paired difference here; equivalence has not been established.

For the PI's broader coverage question, use the existing data to report matched naming opportunities, continuous answer changes on apparently failed swaps, no-Japan sensitivity, and the ensemble-versus-output paired interval. These are inexpensive corrections and clarifications. Prioritize the separate position-split and early-position evidence when deciding whether there is a reader-specific phenomenon worth fresh-data validation; repeating more ensembles on this nearly saturated final-token endpoint is unlikely to change the research decision.
