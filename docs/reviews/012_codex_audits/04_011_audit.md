# Independent audit of experiment 011

Date: 2026-10-06. Author: Codex scientific-review subagent; consolidated interpretation belongs to the parent onboarding report. Scope: Qwen3.6-27B, 82 gated brew development pairs from 33 tables, three emit positions, block outputs L40/L44/L48, SEC scoring; post hoc exploratory AR-derived interventions. All project files remained read-only. No project workflow, GPU experiment, dependency change, or output mutation was performed. Diagnostic Python ran in `/tmp` without importing project modules. This review inspected the original Workspace methods and relevant Oracle appendix, substantive paper notes, 011 design/amendments, raw pair outputs, AR diagnostics, checkpoint metadata, and the small implementation paths needed for specific ambiguities; it is not a repository-wide code audit.

## Main assessment

The current full 011 experiment supports a useful but limited positive result: an additive difference between reconstructed colour vectors shifts the answer toward the predicted partner more than two random direction controls, but less than the model-derived class-mean difference under the tested clean-referenced dose construction. The proposed whitened coordinate swap and its raw-coordinate variant have inadequate positive-control leverage. Those small effects do not establish an absence of relevant information or a mechanistic null.

The handoff's stopped-at-74/unanalysed description is stale. `olens_swaps_s1.json`, `olens_swaps_analysis_s1.json`, and 82 raw pair files now exist. The run has 82/82 pairs, no quarantined rows, and no recorded reproduction failures. The current design records the resumed completion. The historical stopped-run paragraph should be read chronologically rather than as current state.

The additive result is about the local AR loader's directions and this intervention policy. It does not validate the official reconstructor, establish that the unperturbed model naturally reads these directions, or show that the Oracle's verbal report accurately identifies the model's computation. These interventions are reader-derived; they cannot become reader-free causal ground-truth labels.

The additive AR edit flips 4/82 answers, versus 13/82 for the additive class-mean edit. `S` is a normalized log-probability-margin change, not a flip probability or fraction of a known causal mechanism. A ratio of condition medians of approximately one half is descriptive; it is not an estimate that the AR captures half the mechanism.

Both coordinate-swap answer controls fail the stipulated median-S threshold 0.15 by a wide margin. The small whitened effect's interval excludes zero, so “no detectable effect” would also be wrong. The correct reading is a small observed shift with an underpowered intervention for the intended comparison.

The additive class-mean control passes its stipulated median gate. The additive AR answer control also exceeds 0.15 by its point estimate, but its uncertainty spans materially weaker leverage. This is an operational point-estimate gate, not strong confidence that the population median exceeds 0.15. The additive branch's agreed rule gates on class-mean leverage, so that distinction does not invalidate its stated exploratory verdict.

### A definite numerical wording error

`docs/design_specs/011_olens_swaps.md` in the full-s1 verdict says the paired AR-minus-class-mean confidence interval is “below −0.05.” Its actual upper limit is **−0.048670320821240926**, above −0.05. Rounding to −0.05 hides this. The interval is wholly below zero, and equivalence within ±0.05 was not established. It does not establish a deficit greater than 0.05 at this interval level. This is a report wording error, not an error in the saved analysis or its “partial” verdict. Confidence: high.

## What “matched norm” means here

The additive arms match their intended displacement at the clean activation to the class-mean coordinate-reference edit norm, separately at each layer and position. The implementation then clamps a coordinate to `clean_coordinate + k`. Because previous edits change later activations, the realized later write also corrects that accumulated coordinate change. Consequently actual total writes differ across conditions.

Saved median sums of raw write norms across the base-pass sites are:

| Condition | Median sum of realized write norms |
|---|---:|
| Additive AR | 49.1293 |
| Additive class mean | 40.6811 |
| Random additive draw 0 | 90.0734 |
| Random additive draw 1 | 90.2020 |

These are medians of sums; their ratio differs from the median of paired ratios. The median paired AR/class-mean size ratio is 1.0864; AR/random ratios are approximately 0.5670 and 0.5692. Every pair's total random write was larger than the corresponding AR write.

This is consistent with the specified clamp, not evidence of a bug. However, “at matched norm” without qualification overstates the achieved experimental matching. Prefer “with clean-state target edits matched per site, using the same clamp policy.” Larger random writes remaining much less effective weaken a simple nonspecific perturbation account, but do not create a precise matched-dose comparison or establish semantic specificity against output-facing directions.

Implementation evidence: `scripts/011_olens_swaps/olens_swaps.py`, `additive`, `ref_norm`, and `ar_diff`; `scripts/008_trackb/trackb.py`, `Pair.evaluate` (targets from clean activations, writes from current edited activations, `edit_norm_sum_base`). No code was run or modified.

## Specificity and the negative findings

Additional read-only summaries from all 82 pair rows show that the additive AR effect is not solely loss of confidence in the clean answer. The median change in partner-answer log probability is +0.51418 nats, with 63/82 increases; the median clean-answer change is -0.06013. The corresponding class-mean values are +1.26468 and -0.31130. Random partner-answer changes have medians +0.05765 and +0.04709. This makes pure uniform degradation an inadequate explanation of the AR result.

That does not settle whether the AR edit is a content-specific intermediate intervention. The reconstruction training target is the activation immediately before the phrase. These are potentially output-facing colour directions, and brew's own output distribution already carries colour information. A colour direction can influence a related answer without identifying an intermediate representation naturally used by the model.

`top1_retention` has median 1.0 for these arms, but it evaluates a few teacher-forced prompt positions after the first edited position; the code explicitly excludes the answer position. It is useful local damage evidence, not a general-capability test. Off-pair probability mass also changes. No unrelated-task battery is present in this experiment, and no such claim should be inferred from “all gates pass.”

The original two-pair raw-coordinate smoke median near 0.16 does not generalize to the full set: 0.00472. This is a decisive example of why a smoke is an engineering/observational check, not evidence of a recurring scientific effect.

The design's replacement explanation that the whitened null “is because” clean AR coordinates do not favor the true colour is stronger than the evidence shown. Algebra establishes that a two-coordinate swap is along the AR difference, with signed size determined by clean coordinate imbalance. Comparing it to a forced positive additive edit identifies a plausible combination of loading, sign, scale, and downstream clamp feedback. It does not uniquely identify which factor explains the full-sample small effect. Moreover, the full set's summed whitened write norm (10.74; alpha=2: 21.48) is smaller than additive AR (49.13), so amplitude cannot be dismissed globally based on the two-pair comparison.

## AR validation: what is and is not known

The original acceptance rule failed twice. Amendment 2 accepted a changed, explicitly post hoc rule on selected 16-token spans, fitted global scale, and batch-24 retrieval. The scalar is fitted and scored on the same examples. This is a useful diagnostic supporting directional information and the raw-output convention; it is not an independent reproduction of published FVE.

The actual validation tensor contains 768 crops from only 12 generated responses (64 crops each): lengths 1/2/4/8/16 have counts 154/164/137/156/157. The training contract says uniform lengths 1–32. Only 157 crops enter the 16-token FVE, and those are not 157 independent conversations. Random retrieval batches also differ from whatever batches produced the checkpoint's reported metric. These mismatch qualifications belong alongside any comparison to the published approximately 0.22 FVE and approximately 0.93 retrieval.

A common positive global scale and offset genuinely cancel from centered two-vector coordinate swaps: multiplying both columns of V by s divides their pseudoinverse coordinates by s. Unit-normalized additive differences also discard a positive scalar. Therefore the unexplained scalar alone does not invalidate these particular interventions. It remains a warning that the loader may have an unresolved error that is not a uniform scale/offset. Scale cancellation does not rule out anisotropic or input-dependent errors.

Two prior proposed loader problems need qualification rather than automatic adoption:

- The README says the official loader truncates at the deepest required layer, but `heads.pt` includes target layers 20,24,...,60,63. Full 64 blocks may therefore be consistent with deepest-layer truncation. The private loader's behavior remains unknown; 64 versus truncated is a question, not an established discrepancy.
- `meta.json` has `ridge_c: 0.1` **and** `loss_ridge_c: 0.0`, alongside `loss_space: whiten`. The offline diagnostic already swept ridge 0 and 0.1. One cannot conclude from `ridge_c` alone that unregularized whitening mismatches training loss.

The checkpoint also records `loss_mix_lambda: 0.25`, named Neuronpedia J-lens resources, and `val_jloss`/`val_jfve`, while `config.extra.loss_mix_lambda` is empty. These are meaningful grounds for asking exactly what training objective and validation metric ran. They do not by themselves establish that the final loss included a J term. Likewise “joint bullet FVE” need not mean J-space FVE. Direct clarification of the training objective is more discriminative than interpreting geometric resemblance as proof of the objective.

### Why one-word low reconstruction cosine is not “mostly noise”

A one-word phrase does not determine the preceding activation. A well-trained MSE reconstructor can produce a conditional mean with low cosine to an individual target while retaining a useful colour-specific difference. Longer spans specify much more of the surrounding context and should be easier to reconstruct even for a correct loader. Thus one-versus-sixteen-token performance does not isolate reconstruction noise in colour contrasts. The full additive result now supplies positive evidence that the current differences carry behaviorally relevant directional information.

Longer matched phrases remain an experimental idea, not a repair guaranteed by these diagnostics. They could improve colour contrast, or add syntax, task instructions, answer associations, and context. Any such comparison should hold neutral framing constant, compare colour contrasts rather than merely absolute reconstruction FVE, and use the same power and specificity controls. It should follow adequate loader-contract validation if the intended claim concerns the official AR.

## New J-bias diagnostic: a definite control defect and a limited inference

`results/011_olens_swaps/ar_jbias.json` reports AR-to-target dense-J variance-share ratios 1.26533 at L40 and 1.15072 at L44, with its threshold rule returning “ambiguous.” These are modest descriptive shifts in the local loader's output distribution on this crop set. They do not estimate how much Oracle z-likelihood sensitivity can be explained by training geometry.

**The purported PCA-1,024 comparison is not PCA-1,024.** `scripts/011_olens_swaps/ar_jbias.py:63` computes reduced SVD of `htr`, whose verified shape is [384,5120], then takes `Vh[:1024].T`. Reduced SVD returns at most 384 columns; the centered matrix's meaningful rank is at most 383. J and random controls really have 1,024 columns. The script/docstring call all these subspaces 1,024 dimensional, and the JSON has a single `k:1024` without recording actual ranks. Confidence: high, directly implied by shapes and confirmed from the input tensor.

Scientific consequence: comparisons to a supposedly dimension-matched PCA baseline are mislabeled and cannot support that claim. This does not automatically change the threshold-rule “ambiguous” verdict: the J ratios themselves fail its both-layers cutoff regardless of PCA. It also does not erase the measured J/random contrast. Claude Code should consider recording actual matrix dimensions/ranks and using a feasible common rank or a genuinely sufficiently sampled PCA fit before interpreting the PCA control.

The even/odd split also shares all 12 source responses. At least 38/384 evaluation text spans exactly appear in the fit half (38 counts evaluation spans with a matching token sequence, not necessarily identical target position); many others overlap. Hence this is a within-corpus descriptive split, not an independent response-level generalization check. Scales are optimized separately within each evaluated subspace on evaluation targets, so the FVE is an optimized approximation statistic, not a held-out deployment score. The diagnostic contains no uncertainty intervals; random-subspace draws do not substitute for uncertainty over source responses.

Finally, the design's suggestion that the approximately 0.22 J-subspace FVE versus checkpoint `val_jfve` approximately 0.58 establishes that these are “different quantities” is too categorical from the numbers alone. The local metric is specifically raw-coordinate dense-subspace, mean-centered, scale-fitted FVE; the official metric is undocumented. Different definitions are plausible, but loader error, data distribution, span distribution, and fitting convention can also create a numerical mismatch. The current diagnostic cannot rule a training-induced Oracle J preference in or out.

## Highest-value follow-ups for the PI and Claude Code

1. Resolve the exact AR forward/output and training-loss contract, ideally from the authors' loader or one trusted reference input/output and exact validation metric. This determines whether 011 is a test of the released instrument or only a related local construction, and whether Oracle/J agreement has a known training dependency. Avoid spending a long phrase sweep on an uncertain loader.
2. Use existing clean captures and AR vectors to characterize pair/site/layer coordinate imbalances and predicted signed writes, alongside realized edit norms and per-pair margins. This can distinguish weak natural loading/sign from dose or clamp feedback as explanations of the coordinate-versus-additive gap without another model run. The target question is why these two intervention policies differ, not whether any AR information exists.
3. Before treating the J-bias diagnostic as an explanation for 007, repair the rank comparison and distinguish overall energy concentration from recovery of colour contrasts and Oracle readout sensitivity. A modest raw variance-share ratio does not map quantitatively to a large nonlinear reader-likelihood change. A held-out-response diagnostic or same-stimulus colour-contrast analysis is more informative than another arbitrary bias threshold.

All three are specifications or review questions. This audit did not implement changes or launch experiments.
