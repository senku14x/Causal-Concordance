## Observed effects

All entries below are signed fractions of the natural whole-run answer-margin gap, not accuracies or fractions of model

The wider z span improves over the pair-specific 1-D edit in both examples. However, the same-rank PCA comparator has a
larger effect in both, including after matching the actual edit norm. This is evidence on these examples against a
special advantage for the fitted z-span construction. It is not evidence that the intermediate is unused or that no
estimator could isolate it.

The joint span recovers about 63% and 92% of the corresponding full-cell effects. It is not uniformly better than the
rank-33 PCA comparator: joint beats PCA on the first example, while PCA matches or exceeds it on the second. The larger
span is behaviorally consequential here; semantic isolation has not been established.

The 33-table run is needed to see whether these patterns recur. The two smoke pairs are the first two entries of the
frozen manifest, not a random representative evaluation sample. Their many control draws and interventions do not
increase the number of independent problems beyond two. In particular, Pearson r=1 with two nonconstant observations
should not be presented as evidence of reliability.

## Where the added probability goes

The z-span swaps fail the predefined target-directed qualification in both primary examples:

- Table 0009: original intermediate red, donor intermediate black, intended donor answer orange. The z span raises
  orange by about 0.00557 but raises **the original intermediate red** by about 0.0127. Orange receives about 21% of
  the positive colour-mass gains.
- Table 0012: original intermediate white, donor intermediate pink, intended donor answer purple. The z span raises
  purple by about 0.0488 but raises **the original intermediate white** by about 0.125. Purple receives about 23% of
  the positive colour-mass gains.

The donor answer really does gain probability in both cases; the margin effect is not an arithmetic artifact.
Nevertheless, most positive colour gains do not land on that answer, and the strongest gain is to the recipient's
intermediate rather than the donor's intermediate. Thus "we installed the donor intermediate and the model used it"
would exceed the observations. The data alone do not distinguish semantic contamination, partial changes in
computation, or another explanation.

No distribution warning on an edit is not the same as target-directed recovery: the Z rows have no registered warning
flags, yet fail the target-composition criterion. Preserve that distinction.

The clean outside-spelling token inspection supports formatting/end tokens accounting for a substantial part of the
clean excluded mass. It does not identify every excluded sequence event or establish that increases under edited
conditions are harmless. Keep the registered flags; do not tune them to make these examples pass.

## Complement and ablation findings

The directly measured complements expose nonadditivity. For table 0009:

    Z share       = 0.0447
    Z complement  = 0.0525
    full share    = 0.3400
    I             = -0.2428

Both partial edits have small effects, while their combined whole-cell difference has a much larger effect. This is
strong nonadditivity for this example and margin endpoint. It is not a valid partition into independent Z and non-Z
contributions.

The joint complement shares are only 0.0046 and 0.0103, but this does not imply that the joint span accounts for the
entire full-cell effect: the corresponding joint interaction terms are -0.1226 and -0.0171. In the first example, much
of the difference between the full patch and isolated joint-span patch is interaction under these assays.

Z-span mean clamps shrink the natural answer gap by about **14.5% and 11.8%**. The paired-rotation q95 values are
approximately **2.8% and 5.6%**, respectively. This is a substantive improvement over 003's much smaller random-clamp
comparison: the real clamps exceed an edit-size-and-pair-geometry-matched orientation comparison on these two examples.
It is evidence of targeted influence for the fitted subspace relative to these controls, not a calibrated per-item
significance claim, natural-route necessity, or semantic purity certificate.

The start reference is heterogeneous: start-span/full-cell recovery is about **72%** on the first pair and **29%** on
the second. Their median is approximately 50.5%, just enough to make the two-case S3 flag true. Report the individual
results rather than implying the reference was uniformly recovered.

## Fixes before the full run or its interpretation

### 1. S5 uses a different rule from the intended stress criterion

At `scripts/004_span_assay/span_assay.py:969` onward, the implementation uses:

    each fit agrees on whether recovery >= 0.25
    abs(median_recovery_r1 - median_recovery_r2) <= 0.05

This differs in three ways from the agreed clarification:

- It uses S1's 0.25 threshold rather than requiring both stress fits to attain the 0.5 high-recovery criterion.
- It can count agreement that all fits are below the recovery threshold as a successful S5 result.
- It compares the difference of aggregate medians in **recovery-ratio units**, rather than the median of paired
  absolute differences in **S share units**. Signed differences can cancel before the comparison.

Use the explicit criterion discussed before the run:

    median recovery r1 >= 0.5
    median recovery r2 >= 0.5
    median_i abs(S_r1_i - S_r2_i) <= 0.05

Report low-effect agreement separately. Freeze this definition in the design and code before the full run, and retain
the previous smoke summary as an explicitly superseded analysis. On the current two smoke pairs the corrected S5 is
still false, because the stress recoveries are approximately 0.269 and 0.360. The absolute share discrepancy is
approximately 0.0313 and does pass the 0.05 agreement component. No new forward is needed to correct this analysis.

### 2. The full run records fidelity failures but does not enforce G1

`PairRunner.run_all` appends evaluated rows without checking `fid_ok`. `stage_run` records an aggregate `fid_ok` in
`pairs_done.jsonl`, then continues regardless. Analysis also does not reject invalid-fidelity rows. Passing the two
smoke pairs does not ensure all remaining writes pass.

Persist the failing row and diagnostic information, stop on an unexpected failed fidelity gate, and avoid marking the
pair as successfully complete. Ensure resumed runs cannot silently reuse a failed pair as done. Analysis should
explicitly reject or quarantine invalid measurement rows, not treat them as ordinary effects. Test the failure path with
a mocked failure; no new subject-model run is needed for that check.

This is a launch-path defect, not evidence of a bad write in the existing main smoke arms.

### 3. The small-write exemption is broader than the measured resolution

`NEG_REL = 1e-4` skips rho/kappa measurement for any requested write smaller than that fraction of the residual norm.
The saved preflight already finds clean write fidelity at relative size 1e-6. Thus the exemption is 100 times larger
than the smallest tested clean scale. `Fidelity.ok()` also returns true when its lists are empty, and exempt nonzero
writes have no recorded absolute read-back error check.

Eight scored continuation writes were exempted in the smoke, all in random 1-D projection/clamp controls, not in the
substantive Z/joint/PCA edits. For example, a rank-control write of norm 0.000644 on a residual of norm 68.11 has no rho
or kappa recorded. The data therefore support "all checked writes passed, with eight exemptions," not "fidelity
verified on every write."

For the full run, measure ordinary fidelity where the preflight shows it is resolvable, and use an explicit absolute
read-back error bound for genuinely tiny writes. Exact zeros should pass an actual no-op check. Record requested and
realized norms/errors per continuation. Do not simply waive fidelity below 1e-4. This is a limited instrumentation
repair; it does not require changing the content estimator or adding experimental arms.
