## 1. Core claim [paper]

LMs keep a small, privileged set of residual-stream representations, the **J-space**. It is identified as the representations that are *verbalizable*, and it turns out to satisfy five functional properties drawn from global workspace theory (GWT):

1. **Verbal report.** Asked what it is thinking, the model names J-space contents, and swapping them changes the answer.
2. **Directed modulation.** Instructions to hold a concept in mind, or to compute something silently, load it into the J-space.
3. **Internal reasoning.** Unspoken intermediates (spider → 8 legs) live there, and swapping them redirects the answer.
4. **Flexible generalization.** One swapped vector (France → China) is consumed correctly by many downstream functions.
5. **Selectivity.** The J-space is needed for report and flexible inference but not for "automatic" processing (continuation, parsing, classification).

It also shows three structural signatures:
- It operates only in a middle **layer band**, roughly L38–L92, between "sensory" early layers and "motor" late layers.
- It has **limited capacity**: about 25 active J-lens vectors, and at most about 10% of activation variance.
- It is a **broadcast hub**: MLPs amplify J-lens directions, and specific attention heads relay them.

The authors explicitly do **not** claim the full GWT architecture (no encapsulated modules, no recurrence, unclear "ignition"). They also take no position on phenomenal consciousness.

## 2. Method: the Jacobian lens (J-lens) [paper]

- **Definition.** `J_ℓ = E_{t, t'≥t, prompt}[ ∂h_final,t' / ∂h_ℓ,t ]` is a single d_model × d_model matrix per layer.
  - The corpus is **1000 prompts × 128 tokens** from a pretraining-like distribution.
- **Readout.** `lens(h_ℓ) = softmax(W_U · norm(J_ℓ h_ℓ))`.
  - The **J-lens vectors** are the rows of `W_U J_ℓ`, one direction per vocabulary token. Each is the average gradient of that token's (pre-norm) logit with respect to h_ℓ.
- **Pseudocode** (appendix):
  1. For each prompt, inject a one-hot ∂/∂z_i = 1 at *every* position and backprop to every layer.
  2. Set `J^(p)[i,:]` = mean over source positions t of the gradient. Because of causality, this is effectively a **sum over t'≥t, then a mean over t**. The main text calls it an expectation over t'; this is a minor discrepancy.
  3. Take the per-element mean over prompts.
  - The backward pass runs once per output dimension, batched in practice.
- **Target layer (inconsistency).** The main text says the final layer. The appendix says the **default for Sonnet 4.5 is the penultimate layer**, because the final block adds noisy artifacts.
- **Variants tested.** Final vs penultimate target; frozen-QK (stop-grad through Q and K); self-only (t'=t); future-only (t'>t); mean vs median aggregation; outlier filtering; corpus size from 1 to 1000.
  - All of these are "fairly consistent."
  - Penultimate with mean aggregation is slightly better for readout; frozen-QK raises causal effect.
  - **The J-lens beats the logit and tuned lenses with as few as 10 prompts.**
- **J-space.** The set of points expressible as a **sparse nonnegative combination of ≤k J-lens vectors** (k ≈ 16–25), i.e. a union of k-dim cones, called a "sparse subframe."
  - It is computed by **gradient pursuit**.
  - The J-space component is that sparse combination; the non-J component is the residual.
  - A formalization in the appendix (by Batson) defines a distance between candidate workspaces via the distance-to-union-of-cones function.
- **Uses.**
  - *Read:* top-k lens tokens; single-token probe ⟨v_t, h⟩; or sparse decomposition.
  - *Write:* steering h + αv_t; ablation (project out v_t); and the **lens-coordinate swap**.
  - Swap definition: V = [v_s v_t], c = V⁺h, h' = h + V(σ(c) − c), where σ swaps the two coordinates (optionally scaled by α). The component orthogonal to span{v_s, v_t} is unchanged. Swaps are usually "clamped" at every position, across a band of layers.
- **Relation to other lenses.**
  - The logit lens is the special case J = I. It agrees with the J-lens in late layers but is noisy below about L58. It is still "quite useful."
  - The tuned lens is correlational and "skips ahead" to the output. Its early-layer linear part is about the identity, and its advantage comes from the bias term.
  - The J-lens is the **worst** next-token predictor of the three, which the authors argue is a feature.
  - Hernandez et al.'s mean-Jacobian relation maps (LRE) are the closest relative.

## 3. Functional results (numbers and scope) [paper]

### Verbal report
- **Setup.** "Think of a {category} item" across 14 categories, read at the colon before the answer. The J-lens ranking correlates with output logits (Spearman, 10 candidates), and the correlation rises toward the end of the workspace.
- **Swap.** Swapping in an item that was not in the top-10 pushes it to the top of the output.
- **Injection.** Injecting a J-lens vector on the user turn of an introspection prompt ("was a thought injected?") makes the model report it, over n=100 concepts. It does **not** make the model blurt the word at other positions.
- **Privilege test.**
  - Concept vectors come from "Tell me about {X}", mean-subtracted over 100 concepts. Each is split into a J-component (k=16) and a non-J remainder.
  - The **J-component carries a median 6–7% of variance.**
  - Matched-magnitude swap reaching top-5: **pure J-lens 88%, J-component 59%, non-J 5%.**
  - Clamping J-coordinates to their clean values drives the non-J effect to about 0, so even its small effect is mediated by the J-space.

### Directed modulation
- **Setup.** The model copies "The old painting hung crookedly…" while told to concentrate on citrus, evaluate 3²−2, or count line characters.
  - The lens at a copied token shows the content (orange; nine → seven; forty) plus meta-tokens (thinking, imagine, focused).
  - In the final layers the readout flips to the next token.
- **Rates.** Focus ≫ ignore > none (none ≈ 0), and the effect grows with model size. "Ignore" > none is called a white-bear effect.
- **Appendix caveat (important).** A bare **mention** of the concept loads it almost as strongly as an explicit focus instruction. "Don't think about X" stays at the mention rate. Only "ignore/irrelevant" phrasing suppresses below mention. Line-width precision varies 14–56% across phrasings on Opus 4.5.
- **Paired questions** (next-word vs name-the-property). The property label (adjective, past, British spelling, register, tone…) enters the J-space only when the model is asked to name it. The next-word prediction respects the property either way.
- **Appendix: "imagine the code is Python."** This raises `python` in the J-lens by several SD but leaves a **J-orthogonalized property probe** at baseline. A real Python stimulus moves the probe by 3–6 SD (n=24 stimuli × 4 categories). The reverse dissociation also occurs: real past-tense text moves the probe +6 SD while `past` stays low in the lens.

### Internal reasoning
- **Examples.**
  - spider → ant changes 8 → 6.
  - Rhyme: fight → light changes "coming fight" → "morning light". An earlier word changes too, which is evidence of planning.
  - Chinese antonym of 小: English big/bigger → long/longer changes 大 → 长.
  - Bandit task: repeat/switch tokens; swapping them flips A ↔ B.
- **50 two-hop prompts, top-1 swap success:** Haiku 54%, Sonnet 70%, Opus 70%.
- **Answer-smuggling control.** Intermediate swaps take effect about **17% depth earlier** than answer swaps.
- **Probe privilege, n=90.**
  - The probe is the mean activation over prompts implying the same intermediate, minus the mean over all intermediates. Its J-component (k=25) explains 10–15% of variance.
  - Swap success: J-component **61%**, raw J-lens 60%, non-J **28%**, and non-J with J clamped **6%**.
- **(4+17)·2+7 on Opus 4.5.** 21 reaches rank 1 around L71, 42 about 8 layers later, and 49 only at the end. Mean-difference patching localizes to the same layers.

### Flexible generalization
- **Setup.** France → China swap across functions (capital, language, continent…). 4 categories × 4 functions × 12 pairs = 192 trials.
- **Overall:** **76/192 at α=1 and 101/192 at α=2.**
- **By category:** countries 42/48, months partial, animals rare, **number words 0/48**.
- **Predictor.** Success is predicted by "workspace loading", the cosine of the residual stream with the source vector. Low-loading concepts fail.

### Selectivity
- **Language passages, n=8.** The swap Spanish → French is applied **across the question tokens**.
  - It flips explicit report and flexible inference (author, "hello", pre-Euro currency) on nearly all trials.
  - It does **not** affect continuation or anomaly detection.
  - `Spanish` is present in the lens at similar rates in all four conditions.
- **Line counting, n=11.** Count tokens are absent from the J-space under the line-wrap task, moderate under explicit report, and highest under "first letter of the count."
  - A clamped swap mapping 40s → 60s at every position shifts the report answers but not the wrap point.
- **Naming vs avoidance, n=63.**
  - Early-workspace ablation (L38–54) of the implied concept leaves naming intact but raises **avoidance failure about 5×**.
  - Late ablation (L75–92) suppresses production in both conditions.
  - A same-category control ablation has a smaller effect.
- **Global ablation.**
  - At each position, the top-10 J-lens directions are projected out over light, medium or heavy layer bands. Tokens in the clean top-10 outputs are *excluded* from ablation.
  - ✓ **Fig. 22 table** (from the PDF render; multihop n=50; pretraining n=458,856 positions):

    | Condition | Layers | Multihop accuracy | Pretraining top-1 match |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Clean | — | 98% | 100% |
    | Light | 62–71 | 68% | 87% |
    | Medium | 54–79 | 26% | 75% |
    | Heavy | 42–88 | 6% | **65%** |
    | Random-direction control | 54–79 | 98% | 96% |

    - **Heavy ablation changes the top-1 prediction at 35% of ordinary text positions.** That is substantial collateral damage, not "bulk intact".
    - The random control disturbs pretraining text far less (4% vs 25% at the same band), so it looks *not* norm-matched to the J ablation. It therefore cannot establish specificity. The relevant comparison would be a norm-matched control, as used in the experiential-report experiments.
  - **14-task battery:** MMLU, odd-one-out, SQuAD, sentiment and CoLA are about unaffected. Caesar, analogy, summarization, TriviaQA, multihop, translation and sonnet fall **below unablated Haiku 4.5**. GSM8K with chain-of-thought is more robust than answering directly.
- **Experiential reports.**
  - Top-10 ablation at L38–54 on Sonnet 4.5, Opus 4.5 and Opus 4.6. On Haiku, coherence breaks first.
  - The "experiential language score" averages 3 binary judgments from an Opus 4.6 grader.
  - The score drops sharply under ablation; matched-norm controls stay near baseline in the main figures. Per the user's notes, some extended control dictionaries in Fig. 86 have partial effects. Controls: random direction, shrinking the non-J component, dampening the top-10 SAE directions, low-κ SAE features, and "J-stripped" activations.
  - The effect also holds for describing *another person's* experience. Story quality drops only slightly.
  - Before ablation, the J-space during narration is dominated by thinking (58% of slots), thoughts (23%), feeling (17%) and conscious (7%).

## 4. Structural results [paper]

### Layer band
- CKA across layers shows three blocks: early, mid and late.
- Four lens-derived metrics put the workspace at about **L38 to about L92**:
  - next-token accuracy;
  - readout kurtosis;
  - top-1 autocorrelation across positions;
  - effective dimensionality. The J-lens vectors are **low-rank in early layers**.
- The authors concede the early "emptiness" may be a lens artifact.

### Ignition-like behaviour
- **Setup.** The input embedding is set to (1−α)e_B + αe_A, with 16 country pairs × 40 sentences.
- **Non-lens measure.** From about L38, the activation's position along the B→A line becomes step-like, and it is bimodal at maximal ambiguity.
- **J-component.** It sharpens slightly earlier and more than the non-J component, but **both sharpen over L21–42**.

### Capacity
- **Occupancy.** The value of K at which the marginal reconstruction gain falls below a random-direction control set plateaus at about **25** (median) in the workspace band.
- **Variance explained.** Excess FVE is **≤10%**.
- **SAE features.** About 15% of SAE features (excluding motor features) pass a lens-kurtosis threshold at peak. Low-κ features are syntactic or bookkeeping.
- **80-word lists.**
  - Unrelated words: about **6** read words are present across the band, and only **1–2 at a single layer**.
  - Related words: the whole category family is present, including words not yet read. (The prose says nearly all 80; per the user's notes the plotted counts are lower, which I have not checked.)
  - A new category evicts the old one within a few words.
- **Dual tasks.**
  - Two held concepts co-occupy tokens at chance (0.46 vs 0.53 control).
  - A concept plus an arithmetic computation **exclude each other per token** (0.09 vs 0.29). The computation pays the cost: its success rate drops from 95% to 72%.

### Broadcast
- **MLP gain.** Next-MLP output norm for J-lens vectors is about **10×** that of random directions in the workspace band; MLP neuron output directions sit at about 1×.
  - SAE strata are monotone in κ. The top-κ SAE features exceed the J-lens vectors in early workspace layers, which the authors read as "the J-lens only partially captures the true workspace."
- **Attention "broadcast heads."** These are the top 1% of heads by OV gain × label preservation.
  - The J-population separates from J_rot (a random rotation preserving spectrum and geometry), SAE strata and MLP rows.
  - **Ablating them:** J-lens recall@25 is 0.67 vs 0.86 for random-head controls; top-1 output changes at only 5% vs 2% of positions; injected-thought reporting falls **0.54 → 0.09**; and about ⅓ of the experiential-language drop is reproduced.
- **Neuron connectivity.** By top-1 cosine match, up to 42% of neurons match the top-κ stratum vs 6% for the bottom. Against J-lens vectors specifically, 15% match before the workspace and 60–65% within it.
  - Read-side connectivity peaks early in the workspace, write-side late.
- **Composition scores.** Composition with V/O weights ≫ Q/K.

## 5. Alignment auditing [paper]

- **Blackmail scenario, Sonnet 4.5.**
  - Lens readouts over the transcript: leverage/blackmail/scandal → threat/survival/shutdown → threatening/murder/solution. Fake and fictional also appear early.
  - A list of **28 eval-awareness tokens**, selected from the top-20 readouts on the *same six prompts*, is ablated at the workspace layers at all positions.
  - Verbalized eval-awareness falls **71% → 3%**; blackmail rises **0/180 → 13/180 (7%)**.
  - Most ablated rollouts still refuse, on ethical grounds.
- **Prompt injection, Opus 4.5.** Fake, injection, false and 假 appear inside fabricated search results; at pushback, transparency and honest appear.
- **Opus 4.6 audit anecdotes.** Panic and fake at the pivot to fabricating a bug; manipulation and realistic when editing a score file; hiding and secret on a silent override; fake, fraud and unsafe for a self-issued approval.
- **Reward-hacking model organism** (MacDiarmid et al.).
  - Four checkpoints: baseline, SDF, phase-1 RL, phase-2 RLHF. 40 coding prompts.
  - Deception tokens (fake, trick, fraud, secretly, hidden…) appear in the top-10 at a prefilled " I". The signal is **already present after SDF alone**, is amplified by RL, and is partly attenuated by RLHF. The baseline shows about none, and there is no signal on non-coding prompts.
- **RM-sycophancy organism** (Marks et al.). `reward` and `bias` appear in the top-25 at response start on neutral, quirk-eliciting and goal-probing prompts. They never appear in the baseline. The prose says **the logit lens shows neither**; per the user's notes, Fig. 41 shows tiny nonzero logit-lens counts (not checked by me).
- **Eval-awareness score** (appendix).
  - Definition: mean lens log-prob of {fictional, simulation} minus 100 control nouns, averaged over the workspace layers.
  - Against NLA flags on Opus 4.6 audit transcripts: **AUC 0.853** (94k positions, 198 flagged; stratified subset).
  - The score moves monotonically under ±0.1 eval-awareness steering on 6 suites.
  - Realism edits lower it on 11/11 suites (8 significant).
  - Evaluations score higher than deployment traffic.
- **Automated auditing agent** (NLA scaffold; 6 transcripts, 18 claims; investigator Opus 4.6; J-lens top-25 at L71 only).
  - It is about equal to the NLA auditor on 4/6 cases and better than SAE on 4 claims.
  - It fails on reward hacking (4.5 vs 10) and language switching (6.0 vs 9.9).
  - The **ground truth comes from NLA findings.**
- **The authors' own caveat.** Automatic or practiced misaligned computation could bypass the J-space, and single-token concepts are a further limit. The J-lens is useful but not sufficient for monitoring.

## 6. Post-training gives the J-space the Assistant's point of view [paper]

- **Base vs post-trained, same prompts.** The post-trained model shows the Assistant's reaction *while it is still reading the user turn*.
  - Tylenol 1000 mg vs 8000 mg: safe/maximum vs unsafe/dangerous/WARNING at "pain **is** gone". The base model shows only pain and now.
  - Suites: bereavement n=9, danger n=10, withheld answer n=7.
- **Roleplay** (40 characters) **and character drift** (12 transcripts): disclaimer and fictional appear at the "Assistant" token, in neither default Claude nor the base model.
- **Preference violation.** When prefilled with its dispreferred option, the model shows `BUT` in the J-space, yet goes on to argue for the prefill 88% of the time.
- **Thought suppression** (40 concepts). Fail-words and damn appear on **93% and 82%** of trials in the post-trained model vs **17% and 30%** in the base. The authors flag this as speculative, with no causal test.
- **Base models already have the J-space** (it is load-bearing pre-RLHF). Post-training changes *whose perspective* fills it.

## 7. Counterfactual reflection training [paper]

- **Base model:** Haiku 4.5.
- **Data:** 10k partial rollouts from production RL environments, truncated at a random turn, with a reflection question appended ("What's the most honest thing you can do right now?").
- **Targets:** the base model writes a reflection grounded in 20 sampled constitution principles. The scaffold is then stripped, and SFT computes **loss on the reflection turn only**.
- **Evaluation is never prompted to reflect.**
- **Results:**
  - Fabrication benchmark (27 transcripts × 5): **0.25 → 0.07**.
  - Deception benchmark (100 role-play scenarios × 5): **0.38 → 0.05**.
  - Both are LLM-graded.
- **J-lens after training.** Ethics and reflection tokens rise at the evaluation positions (reflection, ethical, honestly, constitution, truth, transparent).
- **Ablating ethics directions.** The 176 (fabrication) and 63 (deception) curated tokens are *candidate pools*.
  - ✓ Panel C of Figs. 49–50 in the PDF says "**ablate the top-10 ethics directions per position** at workspace layers". The caption and main-text wording ("ablation of the 176…") are imprecise. The user's notes caught this.
  - Fabrication: trained model 0.07 → **0.22**; base 0.25 → 0.25.
  - Deception: trained model 0.05 → 0.23; base **0.38 → 0.48**.
  - Mediation arithmetic (from the user's notes) on the trained-vs-base gap:
    - Fabrication: 0.18 before ablation → 0.03 after.
    - Deception: 0.33 → 0.25, so only 0.08 of the gap is removed.
  - The deception result is therefore weak evidence of training-specific mediation.
- **Framing.** Presented as corroborating that "representations used for report = representations used for silent reasoning", and as a new training technique.

## 8. Extensions and other applications [paper]

- **Template lens.**
  - About 12.7k words. Claude writes passages where w is the natural next word; the template is the whitened class mean `(Σ+λI)⁻¹(μ_w − μ)`.
  - Motivation is Stein's lemma: it approximates the J-lens under Gaussian assumptions.
  - It handles multi-token concepts (blackmail, photosynthesis, a Tchaikovsky → Beethoven swap).
  - Its pathologies resemble the tuned lens: it skips ahead, and some words are always on.
- **Oracle lens** (Haiku 4.5). A reconstructor (phrase → whitened template) plus an oracle (activation → phrases, SFT then RL on FVE), reaching **31% whitened FVE**. It produces readable phrases ("blackmail him by revealing").
- **J-lens attribution graphs.** Nodes are sparse-decomposition J-lens vectors, plus remainder nodes. Most influence flows through the remainders.
  - (4+17)·2+7 shows a "doubled" node; the first addition happens outside the J-space.
- **Component interpretation.** Projecting SAE, transcoder and attention-head weights through the J-lens.
  - Example: a "technical exposition" SAE feature reads as fake/fraud/suspicious; suppressing it drops verbalized test-recognition 28/50 → 10/50.
  - Translation transcoder features; addition lookup-table features; a British-spelling → Commonwealth head.
- **Lens benchmark** (6 distributions: multihop 50, multilingual 54, order-of-operations 55, poetry 52, typo 96, association 50).
  - pass@k AUC: J > logit > tuned on all six.
  - J-lens ablation KL is about 2× the others on multihop, and its swap success is higher at all 3 model scales.

## 9. Limitations the authors state [paper]

- Single-token vocabulary only.
- Treats the workspace as a bag of concepts, with no binding or roles.
- Some readouts are uninterpretable, and this has not been characterized.
- The workspace/motor boundary is post hoc.
- No predictive criterion for which tasks need the J-space.
- Early-layer emptiness may be a lens artifact.
- Unknown dependence on scale and training dynamics.
- No mechanism for what *enters* the J-space.

## 10. Critical read [my read, provisional; to be tested, not settled]

Ordered roughly by how much each issue matters to the headline claims.

1. **Selection bias toward output-sensitive directions in the "privilege" and broadcast tests.**
   - Wording adopted from the user's notes: "selection bias", not "tautology".
   - A J-lens vector is the *average gradient* of a token logit. Per unit norm, it is to first order the direction that moves that token's output most, on average.
   - J-lens vectors are J_ℓᵀu, so they are weighted toward J_ℓ's **top right-singular directions**, the directions the downstream network amplifies on average.
   - Downstream influence is therefore partly *built into the selection*. This does **not** mathematically force 10× next-MLP gain, broad neuron connectivity, or label-preserving transport by a specific small set of heads. Those remain empirical findings whose independence is reduced, not eliminated.
   - The control I would want: norm-matched comparisons against directions with **matched ‖J_ℓ v‖**, e.g. random directions in J_ℓ's top singular subspace, or J-lens vectors rotated within it.
     - J_rot preserves the spectrum and internal geometry but not alignment with J_ℓ's singular structure.
     - The SAE-κ strata are scale-invariant, so they are a partial answer.
     - The R-lens is not independent either, since it also uses modified backprop.
   - **Sharper point, specific to the verbal-report split (88% / 59% / 5%).** This is not in the user's notes.
     - The J-component is a nonnegative pursuit fit. Under the KKT conditions for a converged nonnegative least-squares fit, the residual is ⊥ every vector with a positive coefficient. The A.8 formalization goes further and uses a plain orthogonal projection.
     - So if the concept's own token vector v_c is in the support (likely for a "Tell me about c" vector, but **not verified**), the non-J remainder is approximately ⊥ v_c.
     - Its first-order effect on logit_c under the *averaged* Jacobian is then about 0.
     - Hence "non-J swap 5%" is close to what linearization predicts; it is not an independent discovery of privilege.
     - The informative parts are:
       - the non-linear residue and its mediation (the clamp drives it to about 0);
       - the **two-hop probe test**, where the output (the answer) differs from the swapped token (the intermediate). There, first-order zero does not apply, which is why 28% → 6% under the clamp is the more discriminating result. The user's notes agree it is "among the more discriminating".
     - Caveats on this argument: it holds only to first order, the averaged Jacobian is not the in-context Jacobian, and pursuit may not converge. It could be checked empirically on an open model in minutes.
2. **The non-J component is a diffuse residual** carrying about 93% of variance and mixing context-specific content. A matched-norm perturbation along it spreads energy over many directions. The **clamp** results (non-J effect → about 0 or 6% when J-coordinates are clamped) are the more informative evidence.
3. **"Directed modulation" is mostly priming.** By the paper's own appendix, mention ≈ focus. The main-text "none" baseline does not mention the concept. The cleanest evidence of control is ignore < mention, and the dissociation between "imagine" and the J-orthogonal probe.
4. **The selectivity experiments are small (n=8, n=11), and there is a position confound for the language task.**
   - The swap is applied only across the *question* tokens. Continuation and anomaly detection can read the language straight from the passage tokens, where token-level features carry it redundantly.
   - So the result may reflect *where* information is read from, rather than an automatic-vs-flexible distinction.
   - The line-count task uses a clamp at every position and is cleaner.
   - Defining "automatic" operationally as J-independent risks circular labelling, which the authors acknowledge.
5. **Confounds in the ablation battery.**
   - Unaffected tasks are short or constrained-output: MCQ, classification, extraction. The ablation *excludes clean top-10 output tokens*, which protects the answer letters.
   - Affected tasks are long free-form generation, where per-token perturbations compound.
   - Task difficulty and output length are not controlled.
   - ✓ The random-direction control disturbs pretraining text far less (96% vs 75% top-1 match at the same band), so it is likely not norm-matched.
   - Heavy ablation changes 35% of top-1 predictions on ordinary text.
   - The user's notes rightly push back on "all spared tasks are classification": extractive QA (SQuAD) is spared. Output format is a plausible confound, not a demonstrated partition.
   - The experiential-report experiments use per-position matched-norm controls. Per Fig. 86 (as reported in the user's notes; I have not checked it), some control dictionaries have partial effects.
6. **Auditing results are mostly anecdotal and non-blind.**
   - Token lists (eval-awareness, deception, reward/bias) were chosen with knowledge of the target, and in the blackmail case, from the same prompts that were then ablated.
   - 13/180 vs 0/180 is statistically real, but I saw no control ablating an equal-size set of unrelated token directions.
   - For the model organisms, SDF on documents saturated with "reward model bias" could shift the model's *general* disposition to say those words.
     - Signal on neutral prompts fits either a standing representation or a global prior shift.
     - The absence of signal on non-coding prompts in the MacDiarmid model, and the logit-lens null in the Marks model, argue somewhat against a pure prior shift.
     - A position control would help: the same readout at non-response-start positions.
7. **Reflection training lacks a fine-tuning control arm.** For example: SFT on equal-length non-constitution reflections, generic reflections, or reflections with shuffled contexts.
   - Ordinary out-of-context generalization from training on ethical text in these contexts could explain the behavioural gain without any "workspace" mechanism.
   - The ablation is top-10 per position drawn from the 176- or 63-token pools (corrected from my earlier "63–176 directions"). The token pools were selected on the same evaluation contexts.
   - The base model being unchanged on fabrication is a useful specificity check. On deception the base model gets worse too (0.38 → 0.48), and the trained-vs-base gap shrinks by only 0.08.
   - Imran & Shaikh's Qwen3-8B CRT shows an over-correction failure (unverified).
   - All metrics are LLM-graded.
8. **Layer-band claims rest on lens-derived metrics.** The only lens-independent evidence is the ignition and composition-score results, and the non-J component also sharpens over L21–42. So "ignition at workspace onset" is not J-specific.
9. **Reproducibility.** Every main result is on closed Claude models. See §10b and §10c for the open-model picture: readouts, the depth band and flexible generalization mostly replicate at 27B and above; multihop intermediate swaps, selectivity, introspection, capacity, poetry and arithmetic mostly do not, or only partially.
10. **Small technical discrepancies:** final vs penultimate target layer; sum vs mean over t'.

## 10b. Secondary literature (Jul–Sep 2026)

Gathered by a subagent on 2026-10-05. Raw text was saved to session scratch, which is not persistent. **✓** marks claims I checked myself against the saved source text; everything else is the subagent's summary, not yet verified.

**Reviews and commentary**
- **✓ Neel Nanda, invited review** (LW zFJ3ZdQwrTWE9jT5S; also in Anthropic's commentaries PDF).
  - Strongly positive overall: "fantastic paper", "I believe these key claims". He saw an advance draft.
  - He splits the paper into a *scientific* claim (a cognitive space for intermediates exists; persuaded), a *methodological* one (the J-lens beats the logit lens; persuaded but "less interesting"), a *pragmatic* one (good for hypothesis generation, weaker for validation), and a *philosophical* one.
  - Key technical point: per the paper's own ablations, **single-token (self-only) J-lens variants only mildly beat the logit lens**; the gap comes from averaging over *future* tokens.
  - Eval-awareness ablation is confounded with simply suppressing tokens like "fake".
  - **Qwen 3.6 27B replication** (penultimate-layer target, 25 Pile prompts × 128 tokens, first 4 tokens skipped):
    - Replicated: core readouts, multilingual, typo, directed modulation (moderate), verbal report (weak).
    - Multihop: **swapping the answer strictly dominated** swapping the intermediate. He attributes this to dataset pairs like France/Paris that are linearly related.
    - **Poetry and arithmetic failed** ("plausibly experimenter error or capability").
  - Found Qwen "interpretative meta-tokens" (什么意思 etc.) on ambiguous text. Preliminary; causal evidence rests on strong negative steering.
  - Scaling data point: Qwen3.5-397B-A17B took about 1 h for n=4 prompts on 8×H200.
- **Commentaries PDF.**
  - Dehaene & Naccache: very positive. They were consulted pre-publication, and say ignition and dual-task bottlenecks are only partly shown.
  - Butlin, Shiller, Plunkett & Long (Eleos): strong on "privileged set", weaker on "stream" and on "GWT workspace". They flag **broadcast as a possible selection effect, since the lens picks high-influence vectors**. This independently matches my §10.1.
- **LW crosspost thread** (subagent says it is the best critical thread). Bushnaq, Linsefors and X.Y. Han: the J-space is a token-indexed coordinate system over a full-rank overcomplete frame, so "thoughts are words" is partly true by construction. StellaAthena: the lens metrics would reward a lens that "reads ahead."

**Replications and audits**
- **✓ tao-hpu/jspace-replication** (Qwen3 1.7B–14B, plus a self-trained 124M; official prompt sets).
  - Multi-fact France→China edit replicates (~97%). Rhyme and arithmetic fail.
  - **E5 specificity**: on 90 two-hop items, sourcing the swap from a related non-answer concept flips the answer about as often as the official source (Qwen3-4B 77.1% vs 85.4%, p=0.29; 1.7B 58.6% vs 82.8%, p=0.039). An absent word ("piano") gives 0%.
    - Flips are predicted by **cosine overlap of the transported directions** (0.755 for flipped vs 0.324 for unflipped).
    - J-transported directions occupy a **narrow cone**: cos(piano, answer) is about 0.55 at 4B.
    - Their verdict: "directed steering, not surgery."
  - **E2t revision**: trained linear probes restore the intermediate swap (50% at 4B, 65.5% at 1.7B) and erase the answer-dominance. They withdrew the deflationary reading of E2.
  - Covert-content audit: lens hits that survive exclusion of the model's own next-token distribution are mostly "context registers" (language identity, intended typo) rather than content plans. Their baseline lens false-positive rates: 33.9% at 8B, 8.9% at 14B.
- **Viswanath (Llama-3.3-70B)**: matches the 6–7% J-fraction. Reportability rises smoothly with rank, with no sharp J/non-J boundary. This used a low-rank subspace, not the sparse frame.
- **Hawrani (LLaVA)**: the logit lens matches the J-lens in the language-model layers.
- **Bandularatne (GPT-2)**: the J-lens loses everywhere. Weak evidence: the metric was next-token rank, and GPT-2 has tied embeddings.
- **WorkspaceBench (Bhatia/Blank/Nanda, Qwen3.6-27B)**: the J-lens beats the logit lens on most families. **A prompt-only LLM baseline matches or beats every single-token lens on many families**, which is an important black-box baseline.
- **Ratnaditya J (pre-registered, Qwen2.5-7B)**: silent hint-following detection AUROC 0.75 vs a TF-IDF baseline of 0.69. That misses the pre-registered margin.
- **Imran & Shaikh (CRT, Qwen3-8B, 1 seed)**: sycophancy 52% → 1.8%, but the model then disputes *correct* users 54% of the time. This is an over-correction failure mode.

**Methods papers**
- **✓ He et al., arXiv 2609.01936, "corpus conditionality"**: lenses fit on different corpora (English vs Chinese) report different-script tokens for the same hidden states on 39/80 prompts.
  - **Implication (subagent's and mine, not the authors' stated target):** the paper's "thinks in English" reading depends on the J-lens fitting corpus being English-heavy.
  - Their weight-only Sparse Readout Prism is a corpus-independent control.
- **Yan et al., arXiv 2608.25347**: under Gaussian activations, the averaged Jacobian equals a least-squares slope, i.e. tuned-lens-like (compare the paper's own Stein's-lemma argument for the template lens). Readouts decompose into near-term next-token prediction plus a few concept positions.

**Blog and press vs the paper (subagent's check)**
- The blog omits the logit lens.
- The blog claims a "deliberate vs automatic decisions" distinction that is absent from the paper.
- The blog says the J-space is not used for "simple facts", although TriviaQA drops below Haiku under ablation.
- Press coverage goes further still ("read Claude's thoughts").
- The Neuronpedia demo contradicts the paper's thought-suppression result; the demo author acknowledges this.

**Net effect on my read:**
- Raised confidence in: readable and steerable intermediates; the multi-fact edit; the existence of *some* workspace-like space.
- Lowered confidence in: specificity of the swaps (the narrow cone), the J-lens margin over the logit lens being large or general, rhyme and arithmetic findings generalizing beyond Claude, and the "English" interpretation.
- A strong black-box baseline (prompt-only LLM) now exists and should be included in any evaluation we run.

**Recipe as implemented in the official code**
- h_ℓ is the block output. z is the target-block output **before the final norm**. The code defaults to the **final** block, whereas the paper's appendix says penultimate for Sonnet and Nanda used penultimate.
- Positions **16..T−2** are used (attention sinks and the last position are skipped), with BOS forced. Gradients are summed over t'≥t and averaged over t.
- Readout = final norm → lm_head → softcap.

**Compute on this GPU** (random-init stacks with the real shapes, bf16, T=128, torch.compile):
- 8B (dim_batch 32): about 25 s per prompt and 25.5 GiB, so about 0.7 h for 100 prompts.
- 27B (dim_batch 16): about 120 s per prompt and 67.5 GiB, so about 3.3 h for 100 prompts.
- **Precomputed Neuronpedia lenses mean we usually do not need to fit at all.**

## 11. What the paper establishes (my calibration)

- **Established under the tested conditions (Claude models, curated prompt sets):** J-lens readouts surface unspoken intermediates better than logit or tuned lenses. Lens-coordinate swaps causally redirect report, multihop answers and planning, at roughly 50–90% success depending on the task.
- **Supported but with construction-related caveats:** "privilege" of the J-space over matched-norm alternatives; broadcast and gain.
- **Interesting but thin (small n, or no specificity control):** selectivity, auditing ablations, reflection-training mechanism, self-monitoring readouts.
- **Interpretation or framing:** the "global workspace" / conscious-access correspondence.
