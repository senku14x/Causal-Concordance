# 43. 012 Stage E, full multihop run: the Oracle reconstructor's concept differences as edits (2026-10-10)

**Scope.**
- Design: `docs/design_specs/012E_ar_concept_swaps.md` §2–§9, as amended in §15 and §16 (`fa6e874`, written before
  this run).
- Labels: EXPLORATORY. The coverage rescue and every class-mean-sized result on rescued concepts are POST HOC.
- Setting: 82 multihop items (012's cohort), Qwen3.6-27B, Jan's `ModelRunner` (bf16, `BandEditor`, float32 final
  logits), L36–47, every prompt position, prefill only.
- Numbers: every number comes from `results/012_reader_concordance/analysis/stageE_step4.json` (`full`), rounded
  half-up by script. Δm is the change in the margin toward y_b, in nats; a flip means y_b appears in the greedy output.
- Clusters: 31 relation clusters.
- Stopped here for the PI.

## Bottom line

1. **The Oracle's concept differences move the answer.** Adding AR(tgt) − AR(src) at the J-row swap's own edit size
   flips 37/82 answers (45%). Every null (random and J-complement random, two draws each) flips 0/82. The J-row
   reference flips 46/82.
   - At the class-mean edit size, whose placement does not use J rows, the same edit flips 30/82 (nulls 0/82). So the
     effect does not depend on J-row-shaped placement (added 2026-10-10, PI).
   - AR_full passes the absolute bar (≥ 30%, ≥ 20 points above its null) and the power check.
   - **Rung:** a causal claim scoped to these items, edits, layers and sizes.
2. **Removing their J component removes the effect.** With the component in 007's dense J subspace removed at each edit
   layer, the same edit flips 0/82. Its margin effect falls from a median +5.25 to +0.42 nats (nulls +0.14 to +0.37).
   - Removing a random 1,024-dim subspace instead leaves the effect intact: 41/82 flips, median +6.09.
   - **§9 J reading:** (full − J-removed) exceeds (full − random-removed) by +7.85 nats [3.89, 10.82] at J size, and
     by +6.31 [3.17, 8.75] at class-mean size.
   - Since AR_full clears the flip bar, the pre-stated label is **J-attribution (lever)**.
   - *(Corrected 2026-10-10, PI.)* As first written, this said the handle "runs through" J. The comparison behind that
     was J removal against removing a random subspace of the same dimension, which takes out about a fifth of the
     vector's energy (a third of what J removes). Until an energy-matched control was read, the supported statement was:
     **removing J removes the effect; removing a random subspace of the same dimension does not.**
   - **That control is now in (report 44, §17).** A random removal matched to J's energy share (median 0.579) keeps the
     whole effect: 45/82 flips. So the J reading holds under an energy-matched control. The Δ-matched PCA-k arm could
     not be energy-matched (report 44).
3. **The primary is a "weak handle", not a lever.** The J-removed AR arm beats the J-complement null on the margin
   (+0.29 nats [0.08, 0.46] at J size; +0.30 [0.01, 0.53] at class-mean size), but flips nothing (0/82). **So the
   Oracle's vectors do not give the non-J lever report 40's S1 asked for.** The circularity stands.
   - *(Added 2026-10-10, PI.)* The relative effect's mean is +0.29 nats, but its median is +0.10.
   - It is carried by the named-place and Japan items: +0.48 and +0.62. Other bridges give +0.02, non-Japan targets
     +0.13; both CIs include 0.
4. **It is a named-place, mostly-Japan phenomenon.**
   - AR_full@J flips 37/49 named-place bridges and 0/33 other bridges.
   - It flips 27/27 Japan targets and 10/55 others.
   - The J-row lever has the same profile (`stageA_splits.json`). So the Oracle's vectors work where J rows work.
5. **The answer-concept control works as well as the bridge edit.** AR(y_b) − AR(y_a) flips 37/82, the same as
   AR(tgt) − AR(src). These edits therefore cannot separate "the model uses the swapped bridge" from "the edit pushes
   the answer". That is the structural limit 012 already hit with J rows.

## 5. Readings

- **Observation → causal (scoped).** On these 82 multihop items, Qwen3.6-27B, L36–47, every position: adding the
  Oracle reconstructor's concept difference changes the answer on 45% of items. Every random and mismatched control
  changes none.
- **Causal (scoped).**
  - Removing the difference's component in 007's dense J subspace at the edit layer removes that effect: 0 flips,
    margin near the null.
  - Removing a random 1,024-dim subspace does not. That removal takes out about 0.20 of the energy, against J's about
    0.58.
  - *(Corrected 2026-10-10, PI.)* This said "the Oracle's concept handle runs through J" before an energy-matched
    control.
  - Report 44 (§17) has one now: a random removal matched to J's energy share keeps the effect (45/82). So, scoped to
    these items, sizes and layers, **the active part of these edits lies in J at the edit layer, under an
    energy-matched random control.**
  - It does not show that the model computes only through J: a non-J lever might exist that the Oracle's vectors don't
    contain.
- **For S1 (report 40):** the Oracle's vectors don't provide a non-J lever. Without J they move the margin by about
  0.3–0.5 nats and never change the answer. The circularity of J-row-based labels for testing J-sensitive readers
  stands.
  - The model's own class-mean directions don't provide one either. They are a weak lever with J (10/51) and none
    without it (0/51).
- **Bridge vs answer (S2).** The answer-concept edit flips as often as the bridge edit (37/82 each). The most
  answer-facing inputs (single words, templates, "starts") are the strongest. So these levers do not isolate use of the
  hidden bridge from a direct push on the answer.
- **Where it works.** Named-place bridges (37/49) and Japan targets (27/27); other bridges never flip (0/33). This is the
  J-row lever's profile. With 27 of the 37 flips on Japan targets, "the Oracle's vectors work" largely means "they work
  on the Japan items".
- **The TA's whitened swap** works on multihop: 40/82 flips, with a larger, unmatched edit. On brew in 011 it did
  nothing.

## 7. Caveats

- **Scope:** 82 items, one model, L36–47, every prompt position, two edit sizes. The Japan concentration (27 of 37 flips)
  and the named-place restriction limit generality. Bridge type and target identity can't be separated: every Japan
  item has a country bridge.
- **Post hoc material:**
  - the class-mean sizes for the 13 rescued-concept items come from POST HOC easy-prompt class means, whose style
    differs (report 42);
  - the 31 uncovered items use a fixed size profile;
  - results are reported separately for each.
- **Edit sizes are matched at the clamp target.** Realised re-application at later layers differs by arm (report 42
  §5): the random nulls receive more total edit.
- **Dense J vs sparse J:** "J" here is 007's dense 1,024-dim subspace throughout, not the sparse k = 25 part (008).

## Next (for the PI)

1. **The S1 result is negative for the Oracle's vectors.** A non-J lever has to come from elsewhere. Candidates:
   - the class-mean split on a prompt family with stronger class means;
   - DAS-style learned directions constrained to the J-complement (a new instrument, so a design question).
2. **The bridge-vs-answer confound (S2)** is now the binding limit for every lever we have on multihop. Either:
   - a family where the hidden step is not answer-facing (the hidden user-attribute idea, after an output-facing
     check); or
   - an answer-held-fixed contrast. For example, two bridges that lead to the same answer, where a bridge swap should
     move readers but not the answer.
