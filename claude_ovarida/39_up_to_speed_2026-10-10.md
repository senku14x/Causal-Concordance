## 1. The paper in my own words

**What it builds.**
- **The J-lens matrix.** For each layer ℓ, J_ℓ is the Jacobian of the final (or penultimate) residual at every later
  position t′ ≥ t with respect to the layer-ℓ residual at t. It is averaged over positions and about 1,000
  pretraining-like prompts.
- **J-lens vectors.** The rows of W_U J_ℓ give one direction per vocabulary token: the activation's average,
  linearised push toward saying that token now or later.
- **Readout** is softmax(W_U · norm(J_ℓ h)).
- **The J-space** is the set of sparse (k ≲ 25), non-negative combinations of these vectors: a union of cones, not a
  subspace. An activation's "J component" is found by gradient pursuit.
- **The workhorse intervention is the coordinate swap:** h′ = h + V(σ(c) − c), with c = V⁺h. It edits only
  span{v_s, v_t}.

**What it claims** (Claude Sonnet 4.5 unless noted). Five functional properties and three structural ones.
- **Verbal report.** Lens swaps move the reported item into the top 5. In the privilege test (Fig. 8) a "Tell me
  about X" concept vector is split into its pursuit J component and the rest. The J component drives report at a
  small share of the variance; the rest barely does, and what it does is removed by clamping J coordinates.
- **Directed modulation.** Instructions can put a concept into the J-space. A.10 qualifies this: mere mention primes
  almost as much as an instruction to focus.
- **Internal reasoning** (§3.3). Swapping intermediates flips two-hop answers 54/70/70% (Haiku/Sonnet/Opus 4.5), about
  17% of depth earlier than answer swaps.
- **The second privilege test** (Fig. 16). Lens-free probes for 90 two-hop intermediates are split into J and non-J
  parts. Swapping the J part works like the raw lens swap; the non-J part works on 28%, and that falls to 6% when J
  coordinates are clamped.
- **Flexible generalisation:** 76/192 at α = 1.
- **Selectivity:** report and flexible inference depend on J, but continuation and line-wrapping do not.
- **Structure:** a middle-layer band, about 25 active vectors, and preferential amplification and transport by MLPs
  and heads.
- **Applications:** auditing, model organisms, and counterfactual reflection training.

**Two multi-token readers (A.9).**
- **The template lens** is a whitened class-mean direction per word.
- **The oracle lens** is built in four stages:
  - a phrase → preceding-activation reconstructor, trained with **cosine error in the whitened metric**, which is
    scale-invariant;
  - a 3.4M-phrase dictionary;
  - non-negative OMP teacher labels;
  - an oracle trained by SFT and then RL on whitened FVE, with the reconstructor frozen.
  It reaches 31% FVE on held-out assistant activations. The authors argue it confabulates less than an NLA because its
  reconstructor is frozen and linear in templates.
- **Our Oracle Lens is WSB's reproduction**, not a Karvonen oracle. It has an AR (whitened MSE), an inverter, a
  warm start on OMP-chosen rollouts, and RL on FVE with the AR frozen (`docs/sources/web/wsb_post.md:341-350`).

**Text that exists only in the figures.**
- **Fig. 8, middle.** The bars read about 87% / 55% / 9% / 1% (pure J vectors / J component / non-J / non-J with J
  clamped). The prose says 88 / 59 / 5 / "falls to zero".
- **Fig. 8, right.** It takes each condition's best steering strength from a sweep (0.02 / 0.04 / 0.16 / 0.32), which
  is a per-condition selection. The left panel's worked example ("thunder") has a J component holding 12% of the
  variance, against the prose median of 6–7%, and labels the method "matching pursuit".
- **Fig. 16** gives the J part as "16% of probe variance on average", where the prose says 10–15%. It also has a bar the
  prose omits: the J component with the orthogonal complement clamped still flips 54%. That is the sufficiency half of
  the §3.3 test.
- **Fig. 64E** bolds "Golden Gate Bridge" as content "not recoverable from the surface tokens", but the phrase is in the
  prompt.
- **A.21's AUC of 0.853** for the J-lens eval-awareness score is measured against NLA-flagged positions. That is
  agreement with another reader, not ground truth, and it is the paper's own instance of the circularity behind
  Neel's Q4.

**What it does not show.**
- Nothing about phenomenal consciousness or the full global-workspace architecture.
- Not that the lens's "workspace" is the model's. The J-space is construction-dependent (k, pursuit), and early-layer
  emptiness may be a lens artifact.
- No reliability statistics for any reader as a detector, and no false-positive rates for any auditing readout.
- No causal or baseline test of the oracle lens (illustrative examples only; two panels are best-of-5 by FVE).
- No reader-versus-reader validity against independent truth.
- No control for the "broadcast" claim: the directions are selected through the very components whose gain is then
  measured.
- Nothing beyond Claude models. On Qwen3.6-27B, Nanda's and Zeisler's replications found weak intermediate swaps
  (6–11% top-1 flips; answer swaps dominate).
- **The report privilege (§3.1) is partly by construction.** J vectors are chosen for their output effect on the token
  being reported, so at matched norm they favour report. §3.3's probe split is the stronger test.

**Neel's five questions, where each stands.**
1. **What do the readers agree on?** Agreement is high where the content is output-facing or inferable from the prompt
   (012: d_r 0.75–0.98; CW-14: both name the step on 61 of 70 prompts). It is weak on brew's per-pair likelihoods
   (r 0.24 / 0.30) and moderate on naming (κ 0.36–0.63). **Status:** measured descriptively in several settings. No
   formal split into shared signal, shared error and shared prompt inference has been done.
2. **What does one catch that the others miss?**
   - At equal opportunity, Oracle-only beats NLA-only 7 to 2 on working swaps.
   - The NLA alone reports the prompt's *form* (CW-15).
   - On planted concepts the probe catches 11 that nothing else does.
   - The prose readers' apparent advantage over J top-10 is mostly the top-10 cutoff (CW-13).
   **Status:** no unique catch survives a paired contrast. Unresolved.
3. **Does the NLA see content outside the workspace?** Tested as "outside the J part".
   - Planted non-J content: the Oracle reads it better than the NLA (P17).
   - Natural multihop: the NLA needs J like the Oracle does (CW-14).
   - Brew `:` and typo: the NLA keeps its signal without J. But on typo the Oracle also names the word from the rest
     (0.77–0.85), and the NLA's rest-of-J naming is plausibly re-solving from the inverted input (§6, S3).
   - Nowhere has content outside J that the NLA reads been shown to be *used* by the model.
   **Status:** no evidence for the hypothesis. The family dependence is unexplained.
4. **Can majority agreement serve as ground truth?** The evidence says no:
   - against J-swap labels, a family ensemble (0.951) does not beat the best reader (0.961) or the model's own output
     (0.969);
   - majority vote is worse than a probe on planted concepts;
   - readers share wrong intermediates on brew (3–4 of 10 tables, 002);
   - the paper's own A.21 validates the J-lens against NLA flags.
   **Status:** answered negatively on these tasks. The decomposition that would say why is not done.
5. **Does ensembling help?**
   - No demonstrated gain on 012 (+0.002 [−0.020, 0.020] over the model's output).
   - On planted full vectors (005 A1) the union gains +0.16 over the probe, at a 4% false-positive rate; the gain is
     +0.02 on the non-J part.
   **Status:** no demonstrated gain against causal labels.

**The binding uncertainty, in my view.**

- The planned J-independent lever (the class-mean arm, 012 spec §4 line 141 and §11) never ran.
