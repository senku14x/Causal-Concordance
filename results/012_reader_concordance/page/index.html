Readers on swapped states

J-lens coordinate swaps of the hidden bridge (e.g. Eiffel Tower → France ⇒ Paris; swap France→Japan) change Qwen's answer on about half of the items. Every reader (Oracle Lens, NLA, J-lens, logit lens) then shifts toward the swapped-in concept, much more when the model's answer actually changed. Most of that tracking is also visible in the model's own output at the read cell, so it does not yet separate reading the bridge from reading what the model is about to say.

1. The lever: J-row swaps move the answer

J ablation at L24–47 leaves the original answer on 0.72 of items (random: 1.00). The swap works at L40–48 too when every position is edited, so 008's brew null is not about the band alone.

2. The swap has a direction

The paper's coordinate swap reflects the src−tgt contrast of the clean state. When the clean state already leans toward tgt (in pinv coordinates), the "swap" pushes toward src. At L24–35, 18 of 82 edits went toward src (2 worked); at L24–47, 14 (0 worked). Everything below compares working and failed swaps within toward-tgt edits (64 items at L24–35: 36 work).

6. Where the change is built, and what readers see along the way (Stage F)

Same J swap (L24–35), but restricted by position. Bars: answers changed (clean-correct items); hollow dot: random-target control.

Editing only the answer token changes nothing; editing everything except it does almost all the work. The model carries the swapped concept from earlier tokens to the answer token itself, so a reader at the answer token under that edit reads a state the model computed, not coordinates we wrote.

AUC for predicting a working swap. Blue: at the unwritten answer token (all-but-read edit). Orange: mean over earlier prompt tokens, which the edit writes directly (all-position edit; interval: relation-cluster bootstrap 95%). Dashed: the model's own next-token output at those earlier tokens.

At the earlier, written tokens readers mostly see the write whether or not the model ends up using it (AUC near 0.5–0.64), and the model's own output there does not move with success either. The NLA at L42 is the one partial exception (AUC 0.72, partial ρ beyond the model's output 0.54); pre-registered verdict "intermediate". At the answer token, the Oracle and NLA keep a moderate association with the answer change beyond the model's own output there (partial ρ about 0.48 and 0.46).

7. Whitened swaps (the TA's suggestion)

The same coordinate swap done in whitened coordinates (Ledoit–Wolf Σ for L24–35, rows as covectors) changes fewer answers: 26/82 against 38/82 raw (random control 3/82); every whitened flip is also a raw flip. Pre-registered verdict: whitening hurts on this family. Readers on the whitened-swap states show the same tracking pattern (Oracle L44 AUC 0.92, NLA L42 0.91). The reconstructor-vector version of the suggestion still waits on the official reconstructor loader.

8. Why the earlier brew swaps failed (Stage H)

008's lens-coordinate swap on brew edited three cells at L40–48 and its answer-atom control moved the answer share by only 0.010. Rerun at every prompt position, the answer-atom control reaches 0.073 at L40–48 and 0.155 at L24–47, with no answers flipped; the hidden-colour swap stays near zero (0.019 at L24–47). Pre-registered verdict: the family, not positions. The J-row edits at the brew answer cell are only 1–2% of the activation norm: colour concepts there load weakly on their J rows, while multihop bridges load enough for every-position swaps to change about half the answers.

Gates and scope

  • Our captures come from the exact labelled runs: greedy text identical to Jan's code on 87/87 items for clean, J and random arms; α = 0 edit bitwise; read position = WSB last-word token on 87/87.
  • Margins in float32 (Jan's scorer uses bf16 logits; identity-clamp floor median 0.001 nats).
  • Scope: Qwen3.6-27B, 82 clean-correct single-token multihop items (49 of them in 007's already-read test half), one read position, the stated bands. Japan is the target in 28 of 87 covered items. Labels mean "an answer effect under this intervention", not "the bridge is the mediator".
